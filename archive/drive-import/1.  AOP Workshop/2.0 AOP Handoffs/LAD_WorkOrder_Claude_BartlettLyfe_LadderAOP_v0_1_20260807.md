## 2. Non-negotiable research standard

### Full-text rule

Read the complete episode transcript, the complete Bartlett–Wong paper, the complete live Ladder and AOP governing documents named below, and the complete text of every scientific source used to support a load-bearing claim.

Do **not** rely on:

- an AI-generated summary;
- a podcast show-note synopsis;
- a search-result snippet;
- an abstract when the full text is available;
- a review article when the relevant primary experiment or analysis is reasonably available;
- a citation copied from another document without opening and checking the cited source;
- a source’s title as evidence for what its body actually demonstrates.

If a full text cannot be obtained after a reasonable, documented attempt, mark the item **FULL TEXT NOT VERIFIED**. It may be reported as a lead or limitation, but it may not carry a load-bearing claim. An abstract-only source must be labeled **ABSTRACT ONLY** at every point where it is used.

### Citation rule

Every substantive scientific claim must have a point-of-use citation. Use author–year citations in the prose and supply a complete bibliography with:

- all authors, title, venue, year, volume/issue/pages when applicable;
- DOI or another stable primary URL;
- verification status: full text / abstract only / metadata only;
- exact page, section, figure, table, or paragraph location for each load-bearing use;
- access date for web-native material;
- retraction, correction, expression-of-concern, or version information if present.

For each direct quotation, verify the wording in the source body and record the exact page or section. Do not quote a quotation found secondhand as though the original was consulted.

### Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. primary peer-reviewed research or the original formal model;
2. authoritative agency or institutional material for definitions, mission practices, or species accounts;
3. systematic reviews or high-quality critical reviews used to establish field-level disagreement;
4. books and expert syntheses for historical or conceptual context;
5. tertiary sources only for discovery, never for a load-bearing scientific claim.

### Grading rule

Grade substantive claims at the point of use:

- **Settled:** established by named published work within the regime claimed.
- **Synthesis:** a defensible organizing inference made by the project or by this report.
- **Frontier:** scientifically plausible but unresolved.
- **Speculative:** an explicit reach beyond the peer-review bar.

Also assign an editorial verdict where useful: **accurate**, **accurate but incomplete**, **overstated**, **incorrect**, **contested**, or **not verifiable from the cited evidence**. Scientific grade and editorial verdict are different fields; do not collapse them.

## 5. Required scientific questions

Answer every question below explicitly. “Not enough evidence” is acceptable when demonstrated.

### The four pillars themselves

1. What exact operational definitions do Bartlett & Wong give for dissipation, autocatalysis, homeostasis, and learning?
2. Are the pillars claimed to be necessary, sufficient, independent, or causally ordered?
3. Why does the paper display eight partial “sublyfe” regions rather than all fifteen nonempty partial combinations of four binary properties?
4. Are the omitted combinations logically impossible, physically impossible, definitionally excluded, or merely assumed absent?
5. Does the autocatalysis definition improperly equate autocatalysis with exponential growth?
6. Does the homeostasis definition conflate passive relaxation with active, internally organized regulation?
7. Does the learning definition bundle record, processing, use, function, and survival/proliferation in a way that becomes circular or diachronic?
8. How sensitive is classification to the declared system boundary and environment?

- open-ended evolution and whether Earth displays monotonic or unbounded complexity growth;
- parasites and the cooperation barrier;

- sterile workers, inclusive fitness, group selection, and the claim that worker genes “get nothing”;
- evolution as associative learning and the PAC analogy;
- convection, BZ chemistry, Gray–Scott spots, and nonliving “replication”;
- oil-droplet chemotaxis and maze solving, including correct attribution;
