## 1 · The headline claim

**Black holes do not constitute a new gallery class. They are the Long Cold end-state profile instantiated *locally and early*, and they have been present in the universe since the first massive stars died.** [synthesis]

Read the black hole on the four axes and the profile is, line for line, the one already written for the end-state in Ch. 10:

| Axis | Black hole | Ch. 10 end-state |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary | causal only, unmaintained, free | causal only, unmaintained, free |
| Drive | zero | zero |
| Memory | undefined — no clock, and the retrievability question is the information paradox | undefined, for want of a clock |
| Integration | formally maximal, worth nothing | maximal, worth nothing |

The consequence is structural, not decorative: **the heat-death profile is not a far-future object.** It exists now, in pockets, in the millions. The Ladder's ending is already scattered through its middle. That reframes Ch. 10 from "the last chapter of the story" to "the limiting case, with present-tense instances you can point a telescope at" — which is a stronger position, because a negative control you can observe beats one you have to extrapolate to.

**Corollary, and the anti-teleology exhibit the Pre-Rungs currently lack.** [synthesis] A star closes a self-regulating loop and holds it for ten billion years. Its terminal state is the object that scores zero on three axes and is exhaustively described by three numbers. The down-escalator is built into the ground register at the bottom, and it is not an exception — it is the default fate of the only pre-biotic thing that ever closed a loop. Any prose implying the climb is what the universe *does* has to walk past this, in the same chapter, on the way up.

**The sharpest single line:** the maximum-entropy object in the universe is also the minimum-description-complexity object. Bekenstein–Hawking entropy scales with horizon area and is the largest entropy that fits in a region [settled]; the no-hair theorems say the exterior is fixed by mass, charge, and angular momentum [settled, in the stated GR regime]. Three numbers. This is the bestiary's "complexity is rented; simplicity is owned" lesson at its extreme, and it is the reason a single summed persistence score would be uselessly, revealingly wrong.

## 2 · Corrections register — errors made in-session and fixed here

Filed per the standard that a report sustaining all claims unmodified is suspicious. Three corrections, two to Ben's picture and one to Claude's.

**2.1 — Claude was wrong about the Nariai limit being a repeller.** [correction, material]

In-session, Claude asserted that Ginsparg & Perry (1983) showed the Nariai solution decays, and concluded "it's a repeller, not an attractor." **Both halves are wrong.**

Ginsparg & Perry is about the *opposite* direction: de Sitter space is semiclassically unstable to the *nucleation* of black holes, via a Nariai instanton. It says nothing about Nariai decaying.

The actual result on Nariai stability is Bousso & Hawking (1998), and it runs against Claude's claim. Black holes of maximal mass sit in equilibrium — both horizons carry the same temperature. And nearly-maximal quantum Schwarzschild–de Sitter black holes were found, unexpectedly, to **anti-evaporate**: they grow. Subsequent work (Nojiri & Odintsov, and later extensions to modified-gravity settings) found both evaporating and anti-evaporating perturbation modes, so the regime is contested in detail rather than settled either way.

**Net: Ben's intuition has a stronger technical referent than Claude granted it.** A black hole whose horizon approaches the cosmological horizon is a named, studied configuration with a live literature, and at least one leading result has it growing toward the limit rather than falling away from it. The correction Claude gave was confidently stated and wrong, and the grade on the surviving objection has to drop accordingly. [frontier — anti-evaporation is a semiclassical result in approximation schemes, not an established fact about our universe]

**2.2 — But the assembly objection survives, and it is the load the argument actually carries.** [synthesis; the arithmetic is Claude's own, see citation register]

Anti-evaporation applies to black holes *already near* the maximal mass. Getting one there by accretion is the problem.

Order of magnitude, computed here rather than cited: with the SdS horizon condition placing the maximal mass at roughly 1/(3√Λ) in geometrized units, and Λ ≈ 1.1×10⁻⁵² m⁻², the Nariai mass for our universe lands around 10²² solar masses — the same ballpark as the total mass-energy inside the present horizon. The largest known black holes run 10¹⁰–10¹¹ solar masses. **The gap is eleven to twelve orders of magnitude in mass, and accelerating expansion is actively removing the material that would close it.** Anything not gravitationally bound is receding and will pass beyond the cosmological horizon; there is no universal merger cascade, only isolated bound pockets each ending with one large hole and nothing in reach.

So the Nariai configuration in our universe is reachable, if at all, by *primordial nucleation* rather than by accretion — which is exactly the Ginsparg–Perry / Bousso–Hawking pair-creation-during-inflation channel. **This routes directly to the PBH loophole already flagged as "handled too quickly" in the dark-sector note.** Same channel, same open question, now with a second reason to reopen it.

**2.3 — Black holes are not currently shrinking, and the reason is not that Hawking radiation is wrong.** [settled]

A solar-mass black hole has a Hawking temperature near 60 nanokelvin; the microwave background is at 2.725 K. The hole is therefore absorbing far more than it emits and is *growing*. Supermassive holes are worse — temperatures of order 10⁻¹⁸ K. Evaporation cannot begin until expansion cools the CMB below the hole's own temperature.

Rough timing, computed here: closing a factor of ~4×10⁷ in temperature takes about 18 e-folds; at a de Sitter Hubble time of order 17 Gyr, that is **of order 10¹¹–10¹² years** before stellar-mass holes even begin net evaporation, against evaporation lifetimes of ~10⁶⁷ years (stellar) to ~10¹⁰⁰ (largest). Flagged as an estimate with the method shown, not a citation.

**This is the answer to "why don't we see them shrink."** It belongs in the Bestiary's "by horizon" entry, which currently gives the evaporation timescales without the reason they are not yet running.

**2.4 — A horizon does not consume spacetime.** [settled] Horizon area grows with absorbed mass; the exterior metric is unaffected. "Eating spacetime and depositing it on the boundary" has no referent in GR. The holographic statement — that a region's description may be written on its boundary — is about *description*, not about the boundary devouring the bulk. And black holes are inefficient eaters in any case: orbits are stable, and in galactic-scale dynamical evolution far more mass is ejected by gravitational scattering than is swallowed.

## 4 · Dissent register

**D1 — The profile identity may be an artifact of coarse axis readings.** "Maximal integration, zero drive, undefined memory" is what you get from *any* system where the instrument's declarations cannot be filled in. If the black hole and the end-state match because both defeat the instrument in the same way, the match is a statement about the instrument, not about the universe. This is the strongest objection in the note and it is not answered here. It also inherits Ch. 10's own admitted caveat, that the integration computation is a quantum-information move being run on an axis whose working default was built for classical and Gaussian systems.

**D2 — "Integration maximal" may be exactly backwards.** No-hair says three numbers. A three-parameter object is arguably at *minimum* integration by any complexity-flavoured reading, and the "maximal" reading comes entirely from the purity-of-the-global-state argument. The note leans on this tension for its sharpest line ("maximum entropy, minimum description") without resolving which reading the axis actually licenses. If the axis cannot say, that is an AOP-facing defect, not a Ladder finding.

**D3 — The anti-teleology exhibit may prove too much.** If the terminal state of every loop-closer is a zero-profile object, that is a claim about stars specifically, not about persisters generally; cells do not end as black holes. Generalising the down-escalator from one case is the same overreach the note accuses the climb of.

**D4 — Anti-evaporation is thin ice to lean on.** It is a semiclassical result in s-wave and large-N approximation, with contradicting perturbation modes found by later authors. Ben's picture gets a partial vindication from a result that may not survive. The honest version is "your configuration is studied and the literature is unsettled," not "you were right."

**D5 — Nothing here is new physics or new to the Ladder.** The Bestiary already lists black holes under "by horizon" and already states the anticorrelation between duration and depth. This note re-files existing material under a sharper frame and adds two mechanisms (CMB crossover, Nariai) and one consolidation. If the consolidation is rejected, the residue is two footnotes.
