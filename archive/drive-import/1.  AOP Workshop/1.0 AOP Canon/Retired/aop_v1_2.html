The Architecture of Persistence

A classified dependency structure among Boundary, Drive, Memory, and Integration, with a semantic mask read out by a system’s own viability

Abstract

Persistence is a thing resisting erasure along several distinct directions at once. This paper names four such directions—Boundary (space), Drive (energy), Memory (time), and Integration (parts)—each a relative-entropy difference along its own axis (three static cuts and one, Drive, a trajectory asymmetry), and asks how they depend on one another. The result is a two-layer classification. The first layer is a coupling graph whose edges are sorted by modal status: some couplings are compelled by physical law, some are conditional, some are free. The second layer is a semantic mask that separates the couplings causally required for continued existence from those merely present, isolated by intervention on a system’s viability. The structure is read out relative to the persisting system’s own viability function and is defined only for systems with a timelike worldline. Energy is the hub, but its spokes are uneven: two are forced and scoped to a stated regime—the thermodynamic uncertainty relation prices reliability, and dissipation forces a memory floor (a corollary of trajectory irreversibility)—while the rest are conditional or free. A structural consequence sits alongside the hub: mask-resolvability and Integration trade off, so the framework reads most cleanly on weakly integrated systems and blurs on the strongly integrated ones it was built to describe. We locate the observer precisely enough to contain the homunculus problem, bound the theory’s domain by a binding criterion (of which rest mass is the corollary), and illustrate the abstractions on five cases spanning full persistence to none (crystal, flame, spore, a bound atom, a free photon). The framework makes a concrete first prediction: because semantic weight is an observer-relative quantity, the measurable it forecasts is not a single weight but the range a weight sweeps as the declared partition, resolution, and viability are varied. The organizing notion — that what survives can be selected by differential persistence rather than differential reproduction — is itself established across scales: it has been argued at the level of lineages and processes, and demonstrated for self-perpetuating feedback systems from autocatalytic networks to ecosystems, belief systems, and economies. This is a synthesis of established results, not new physics; claims are tagged throughout as settled, as this framework’s synthesis, or as frontier.

Keywords: persistence · stochastic thermodynamics · semantic information · biological individuality · entropy production · excess entropy · Markov blanket · causal horizon

1Introduction and scope

The question of what makes a thing a persisting thing—an individual, a system that holds together—recurs across nonequilibrium physics, computational mechanics, information theory, and theoretical biology, and each field answers it with a different quantity. This paper does not add a quantity. It proposes an organizing structure over the quantities the field already has, and asks a question those literatures rarely pose to one another: how do the distinct dimensions of persistence depend on each other, and which of those dependencies are compelled by physical law?

The claim, in one paragraph, is this. Persistence is resistance to erasure along four directions: a maintained separation of interior from exterior (Boundary), a free-energy throughput that holds the system off equilibrium (Drive), information the present carries that constrains its own future (Memory), and the degree to which many parts act as one irreducible whole (Integration). These four are not independent decorations; they are coupled, and the couplings admit a classification—forced, conditional, or free. Over that classification sits a second layer that distinguishes couplings carrying causal weight for continued existence from couplings that are merely present. The deliverable is that two-layer object: a coupling graph with a semantic mask over it, read out relative to the system’s own viability, and defined only for systems that have a present tense to persist in.

Three commitments bound the scope from the outset, and are kept deliberately because the source literatures repeatedly press against them.

Three load-bearing refusals

No ownership. Which aggregate is the “true” individual, or which self does the inferring, is never the question. The framework classifies couplings; it does not individuate. This refusal is not merely cautious: independent attempts to measure ownership directly — to quantify the fraction of its own maintenance a system performs on its own behalf — have failed at exactly this point. A candidate observational measure of self-performed maintenance was found to collapse into an existing quantity (non-trivial informational closure), unable to separate “the system maintains itself while drawing on inputs” from “the environment supplies the structure,” with three independent lines of evidence converging on the conclusion that observational measures cannot make this separation for open systems. The proposed remedy in that literature is to abandon observation for intervention. We take both lessons: ownership is not observationally recoverable, so we decline to individuate; and where a directional judgment about coupling is needed, we intervene (the semantic mask, Section 3) rather than correlate. No metaphysics of selfhood or worth. It does not decide what a self is or what a persisting thing is worth; it describes structure. No master quantity. The four dimensions share one mathematical form—a relative entropy—but each is pointed at a different partition: inside versus outside, past versus future, part versus part, forward versus reverse trajectory. The common form is what makes them commensurable; the different targets are what keep them independent. It is a mistake to read the shared form as the cause of the dissociation—the form would, if anything, invite collapse; the distinct partitions are what force four axes rather than one. The unification is also thinner than it first appears, since nearly every quantity in information theory is a relative entropy: the shared operation is a common language, not a hidden master quantity. It licenses pluralism; it does not license reduction.

Nothing below claims a new law of physics. Every forced edge is tied to a named, published result; every inference this framework makes on its own is flagged as such (Section 12). The contribution, if there is one, is the assembly and its classification.

2The object: four dimensions as differences

A persisting thing holds a difference-that-could-be-otherwise along four directions. Crucially, none of the four is a substance one can point at. Each is a computed difference across a cut—a mutual information—that is never observed directly. One observes residues, bent flows, and locked shells, and infers the difference behind them. The event horizon makes this unavoidable: a horizon casts only a shadow—the dark disk in a horizon image is the critical photon ring, not the horizon itself—and the horizon is inferred from what it does to surrounding light. Every dimension is like this. One gets the shadow, never the thing.

Drive is the odd one out. It is not a mutual information across a static cut but the time-asymmetry of the dynamics: entropy production equals the relative entropy (Kullback–Leibler divergence) between the forward and time-reversed trajectory distributions [1]. So all four are relative-entropy quantities pointed along independent axes—forward-versus-reverse trajectory (Drive, directional), past-versus-future state (Memory, time-symmetric), inside-versus-outside (Boundary), and part-versus-part (Integration). The count is held open—at least these four, not a claim that exactly four exhaust the phenomenon. Two of them, Drive and Memory, are computable with no choices to make. Two, Boundary and Integration, exist only relative to a declared partition: which states are “inside,” which parts are “the parts.” That partition-dependence is a permanent scope limit, stated at the outset rather than patched. Boundary and Integration are the soft dimensions precisely because someone must declare the cut, and the answer moves with it.

Integration carries a second, subtler ambiguity beyond the partition: there is no single canonical measure of it. Six distinct candidate measures of integrated information have been reviewed and compared, and on Gaussian network models no two show consistent agreement across all analyses; some can even go negative [14]. This is an instance of a broader regularity: across more than a dozen independently developed measures of causation, the measures turn out to be near-rediscoveries of a small set of shared primitives, agreeing on structural conclusions (such as causal emergence) while differing in the values they assign [Comolatti & Hoel 2025] — direction survives the choice of measure; magnitude does not. We confirmed the qualitative pattern directly — computing two of the closed-form measures on thousands of coupled Gaussian systems, they agree in rank (Spearman ≈ 0.87) but disagree in value, and whole-minus-sum integrated information is negative for a small fraction of systems. AOP does not attempt to resolve this; it inherits the ambiguity but not its stakes, because it uses Integration as one descriptive axis among four, not as a measure of consciousness. For all worked computations we therefore declare an operational default: total correlation, TC = Σ H(Xᵢ) − H(X) [Watanabe 1960], the member of the family that is non-negative by construction, monotone in coupling, and closed-form in the Gaussian case. Where the synergy/redundancy character of the interdependence matters — not just its magnitude — the O-information [Rosas, Mediano, Gastpar & Jensen 2019] is the natural signed companion, negative for synergy-dominated and positive for redundancy-dominated structure; we note it as the measure to reach for when that distinction is load-bearing. This is a choice of operational proxy, not a claim that it is the uniquely correct measure. Accordingly, every Integration-dependent claim in this paper is stated in its directional form — all reasonable measures rise with coupling — and no exact Integration value is load-bearing. Where "simultaneous segregation and integration" is specifically what one wants to capture, causal density is the measure to prefer [14].

3Two layers: syntactic correlation and semantic mask

The four dimensions as usually measured are syntactic—plain Shannon correlations, which by themselves carry no meaning. Persistence turns on the semantic subset: the correlations causally necessary for the system to maintain its own existence. The distinction, and the method for drawing it, are due to Kolchinsky and Wolpert [2], who define semantic information as the syntactic information a system holds about its environment that is causally necessary for it to keep itself in a low-entropy state, and operationalize “causally necessary” by counterfactual intervention—scramble a correlation, re-run the dynamics, and measure whether viability drops. What survives scrambling was decorative; what reduces viability when scrambled was load-bearing. Two adaptations should be flagged. Kolchinsky and Wolpert define semantic information for the system–environment correlation; applying the same scramble-and-re-run test to a system’s internal couplings, as we do here, is an extension of their method rather than a direct application of their result. And the test presupposes that the scrambled coupling can be intervened on in isolation—an assumption that fails on strongly integrated systems (Section 6).

This is the central organizing move: a syntactic coupling graph, with a semantic mask over it. Classification then means locating each coupling on both axes at once—its modal status (forced, conditional, or free) and its semantic weight (does scrambling it drop viability?). An edge can be present but semantically inert, or thin but load-bearing.

One point must be stated plainly, because it governs how the second layer may legitimately be read. Semantic weight is a quantity, not a law. Like every dimension magnitude in Table 1, it is observer-relative: its value depends on the declared partition, the resolution, and the viability functional chosen, exactly as Integration’s value does (Section 6). The mask is well defined as a construction; its numbers are not invariants. Treating a semantic weight as a fixed, observer-independent coordinate would import a determinacy the framework does not possess and does not claim.

4The coupling graph and its classification

Energy is the hub, but its spokes are not alike. This uneven hub is the spine of the structure.

Drive → Memory: dissipation forces a memory floor. Any sustained dissipation leaves some trace: a strictly positive entropy production rate implies a strictly positive excess entropy (σ > 0 ⇒ E > 0). Here excess entropy is E = I(past; future), the past–future mutual information of computational mechanics [15]; it should not be confused with the "excess entropy production" of steady-state stochastic thermodynamics, an unrelated quantity that lives on the Drive axis. Unlike the brittle earlier reading, this edge is not a fresh directional guess but a scoped corollary of the trajectory-irreversibility identity we already invoke for Drive [1]. For a stationary process described by a single complete state, E = 0 holds if and only if past and future are independent, i.e. the process is i.i.d.; an i.i.d. process equals its own time-reverse in distribution, so its entropy production vanishes. The contrapositive is exactly σ > 0 ⇒ E > 0. Figure DM confirms this on a driven Markov ring: every dissipating configuration carries E > 0, while the single detailed-balance configuration has σ = 0 with E > 0 — the converse fails, a reversible oscillator remembers for free, so the necessity runs one way only.

The implication is secure but scoped, and three conditions must be stated or it is either false or vacuous. First, σ and E must be read on the same complete description: coarse-graining can hide a nonequilibrium current so that a reduced observable looks reversible (its E reads near zero) while the full dynamics dissipate — the Memory-axis twin of the observer-relativity of σ itself (Section 5). Second, the process must be stationary, the regime in which σ = D(forward ∥ reverse) is the clean object used here. Third, and as before, what Drive forces is a memory floor (E > 0), not memory depth: it guarantees that some past–future correlation exists, not that the load-bearing memory is large. The spore is the standing witness — near-zero dissipation carrying deep stored structure (Section 11) — showing that the quantity Drive compels (E) and the quantity that does the persisting work (statistical complexity Cμ) can come apart [15]. Within these conditions the edge is a forced, scoped consequence of a cited law, and it joins the thermodynamic uncertainty relation as the hub's second clean forced spoke rather than a hedged inference.

Drive → Reliability is forced and regime-bounded. The precision of any current a system runs is bounded by its dissipation: the thermodynamic uncertainty relation prices steadiness [4], [5]. Within the classical Markov regime in which it is proven, this is a law.

Drive → Boundary is conditional. Holding a gradient costs nothing at equilibrium—a potential well is free—and costs drive only when the boundary must be maintained against a leak, with the price set by how leaky it is.

Drive → Integration is free at equilibrium, open under selection. Parts can be correlated at zero dissipation; integration exists statically for free. Whether drive builds or maintains integration over time is a separate, unsettled question, and conflating the two is an error.

The three couplings that do not pass through energy—Boundary–Memory, Memory–Integration, and Boundary–Integration—are where the genuine open work sits. The current wager is that they are contingent and dissociable, except where a particular construction welds one. In the leading information-theoretic account of biological individuality [6], the boundary is defined as the partition that maximizes a memory quantity (autonomy), which welds Boundary to Memory by construction. But that weld is imposed by a definition, not compelled by a law—and the flame is the standing counter-witness: a sharp boundary with essentially no memory at all (Section 11). Memory–Integration and Boundary–Integration remain genuinely open, and their answers depend on which contested measure of integration one adopts (Section 6). Table 2 records the classification.

The two edges the earlier ledger marked "open," M–I and B–I, can now be classified, again by borrowing the exact machinery rather than building it: the information decomposition for coupled Gaussian (vector-autoregressive) processes is available in closed form [Faes, Marinazzo & Stramaglia, Entropy 19(8):408 (2017)]. Computing all three non-energy dimensions on one such process, we find Memory dissociates freely from both Boundary and Integration — it is a mutual information on the time-lagged covariance, a different object from the instantaneous cuts, and across random dynamics its correlation with each is below 0.05. Boundary and Integration are dissociable but not free: every combination is reachable by construction (two sealed modules realize high Integration with zero Boundary; a cross-cut correlated pair realizes Boundary with little Integration), yet across generic systems the two are positively correlated (~0.83) because both are static mutual-informations read off the same covariance matrix. The honest statement is therefore graded — M–I dissociable and free, B–I dissociable but substrate-sharing — not a blanket "all three independent," which the random-system test shows would be an overclaim. 5Laws versus quantities: the scale claim, done carefully

A brittle early form of this framework held that “forced means scale-invariant”—that a forced coupling, being a law, holds unchanged from molecules to institutions. That reading breaks the moment one notices that measured irreversibility is itself scale-dependent, and that coarse-graining can create or destroy apparent causal structure [7].

The repaired claim is sharper and survives: the laws are observer-independent; the magnitudes are observer-relative. A forced coupling transfers across scale because it is a law—the second-law inequality σ ≥ 0 does not care about one’s grain. But how much boundary, how much memory, how much σ a system “has” depends entirely on the cut and resolution at which it is measured. The relation is invariant; the number is not. Figure 1 shows this directly: coarse-grained entropy production swings by tens-fold with the observer’s grain, while the law σ ≥ 0 holds at every grain. That entropy production is measured at all in real systems—in living cells [8] and in the human brain [9]—is what lets the forced, law-tied quantities touch reality; the observer-relative magnitudes should stay scoped to a declared cut.

6The two-axis view

Every coupling lives at a point in a plane: modal status (free → conditional → forced) against semantic weight (inert → load-bearing). Persistence lives up-and-to-the-right—couplings both forced and load-bearing. The modal axis alone was never enough, because an edge can be present but semantically inert, or thin but load-bearing. Two knobs, not one.

The two axes are not of the same kind, and Figure 2 must be read with that asymmetry in mind. The vertical (modal) axis is a law axis: forced/conditional/free is observer-independent. The horizontal (semantic) axis is a quantity axis: as argued in Section 3, semantic weight is observer-relative, so an edge’s horizontal position is not a single invariant coordinate but a value that shifts with the declared partition, resolution, and viability functional. The point placements in Figure 2 are illustrative, chosen to convey rank and region, not measured magnitudes. Read honestly, each edge is better pictured as a horizontal range than as a pinned point—the width of that range is itself a diagnostic (how far the weight moves as one varies the declaration), and measuring it is the natural first computational task the framework invites.

A resolvability limit: the mask blurs as Integration rises

There is a sharper structural consequence, and it deserves to be stated as a result rather than buried as a caveat. The semantic mask is defined by intervening on one coupling at a time—scramble an edge in isolation, re-run, and read the viability drop. That procedure presupposes the edges are separately manipulable. But high Integration is precisely the regime in which they are not: when parts are strongly interdependent, no single coupling can be scrambled without dragging the others with it, and the per-edge weight the mask is supposed to return is no longer well defined. The two axes of Figure 2 therefore trade off against each other. The more a system relies on Integration—the more it is the kind of unified, tightly coupled object the framework most wants to describe—the less sharply its semantic weights can be resolved. This is not a defect of the instrument to be engineered away; it is a genuine limit — by analogy (not by proof) reminiscent of an uncertainty relation, a tension between how integrated a system is and how finely its internal semantics can be resolved. We intend the analogy loosely: there are no conjugate variables and no quantitative bound here, and the honest, measurable form of the tension is the one given in Section 13 — the width a semantic weight sweeps as the system's integration rises. It bites hardest on exactly the systems the framework exists to describe, and Section 13 treats it as a first-class limitation rather than a footnote.

This width is now quantified, and — consistent with the framework's method of borrowing established results rather than inventing them — it turns out to be a law already discovered independently in three fields. In a minimal Gaussian model of n interdependent components with a single integration knob (equicorrelation ρ, Integration measured as total correlation), the uncertainty in a per-component semantic weight diverges as (1 − ρ)^(−1/2) — equivalently as 1/√λ_min of the coupling matrix — while the aggregate, whole-system weight simultaneously *sharpens*. The individual parts become unresolvable at exactly the rate the whole becomes better determined. This is the same structural fact as multicollinearity in econometrics (correlated regressors inflate per-coefficient variance while leaving predictions intact; the variance-inflation factor is the same 1/(1−ρ) form), as parameter sloppiness in physics and systems biology (the Fisher-information spectrum spans orders of magnitude; sloppy directions are unconstrained while stiff directions and predictions are tight [Transtrum, Machta & Sethna, Phys. Rev. Lett. 104, 060201 (2010); Transtrum et al., J. Chem. Phys. 143, 010901 (2015)]), and as attribution non-uniqueness in partial information decomposition [Williams & Beer, arXiv:1004.2515 (2010)]. That three independent literatures converged on the same width law is the strongest available evidence that AOP's resolvability limit is real rather than an artifact of the framework; the prediction is not new, which is exactly why it is defensible. 7The observer, located

“Observer” does real work here, so it must be pinned down rather than left as a black box. At every grain an observer does exactly three things: it declares a cut; it reaches the far side only by intervention (information comes only by posing a question); and it has a stake. What physically plays the observer changes by grain—a coarse-graining at the thermodynamic grain, a model partition at the statistical grain, an intervener at the causal grain. At the grain that matters for persistence, the observer is nothing external: it is the system’s own viability function. Semantic information is meaningful to a system, relative to its viability [2]; the frame that fixes which differences matter is the persisting system itself.

This is where a homunculus threatens to re-enter, and the objection deserves a direct answer rather than an assertion. If the system’s viability fixes which differences matter, who evaluates the viability function? If the answer is “we do, from outside,” the external mind walks back in. The defense is a chain, and every link must be explicit. (i) By the present-tense principle (Section 9), a legitimate viability function is checkable now. (ii) A property checkable now is a structural fact about the current state, not an assessment performed by anyone—the configuration either is or is not viable, whether or not a witness scores it. (iii) What plays the evaluator is selection, which is agentless: the frequency of good environments pays out the wager, with no one at the wheel [10]. Here a distinction earns its keep: what is observer-independent is only the bare fact that the current state either is or is not viable—the magnitude or ranking of viability remains relative to a declared functional V, and the anti-homunculus argument needs only the former. Therefore no external evaluator is required for the existence-fact, and the homunculus does not return. The claim rests on the present-tense principle being airtight; where that principle is soft, the anti-homunculus move is soft, and we mark it accordingly. This is containment, not dissolution: the evaluator role is located and bounded rather than made to vanish.

8What can make a boundary: screenability

If a boundary is any physical interaction surface, one must ask which interactions can actually make one. A statistical boundary—inside conditionally independent of outside given the interface—requires the interaction to be screenable, i.e. interruptible. Electromagnetism screens (opposite charges, a Faraday cage, a membrane). The strong interaction confines rather than screens, and the weak interaction is intrinsically short-ranged (its mediators are massive); neither builds a statistical boundary at the scales where persisters live. Gravity cannot be screened: there is no negative mass and its range is infinite, so it forms no statistical boundary at all. It is the anti-boundary interaction (Figure 4).

Figure 3. Which interactions can make a boundary. Electromagnetism screens and so builds membranes; the strong force confines and the weak force is short-ranged, so neither makes a statistical boundary; gravity, being unscreenable and infinite-ranged, forms no statistical boundary—its only boundary is the causal horizon. Gravity therefore imposes an unscreenable floor on Integration: nothing is ever perfectly decoupled.

The single exception is the event horizon. Gravity cannot make a screening boundary, so the only boundary it makes is causal—a one-way membrane fixed by global structure, not local shielding. This is why a black hole is the clean, observer-independent, cannot-see-it boundary, and why gravity floors integration for everything. A consequence worth stating: gravity is syntactically universal but semantically null for most persisters (scramble a cell’s gravitational tie to a distant star and nothing dies), and semantically total for gravitationally bound persisters (stars, orbits, accretion disks), where it is the entire boundary, membrane-free.

The two notions of boundary—the informational Markov blanket and the thermodynamic gradient-maintenance cost—are genuinely distinct, and conflating them is a known error. The information-theoretic blanket is observer-relative by nature: one must first choose which node is “inside,” after which the blanket appears, and the instrumental and realist readings of that construction should not be run together [11]. A single hydrogen atom in a sealed de Sitter patch illustrates that one persister can carry a boundary of each kind at once (Figure 4): a cheap, screenable electromagnetic skin—the electron in its Coulomb well, costing essentially no drive—and an unscreenable causal horizon, its de Sitter patch, which acquires a well-defined entropy only once a witness on a timelike worldline is present to define it [12], [13]. The atom is not merely a guest in the patch: it is what fixes the patch’s observer-relative entropy, the worldline relative to which that entropy is defined.

Figure 4. One persister, two boundaries. A bound hydrogen atom carries a screening boundary (the electromagnetic skin—material, local, probeable, essentially free) and a causal boundary (the de Sitter horizon—non-material, global, unscreenable, inferred from the faint Gibbons–Hawking glow). The horizon’s entropy is observer-defined: it is well posed only once a timelike witness sits inside the patch [12], [13].

9Present-tense viability

Any legitimate viability function must be checkable at the present instant. A viability that requires foresight—“this will eventually reproduce”—is illegitimate; it is hindsight worn as a goal. Persistence maintains; it never aims. Apparent foresight, such as a spore seeming to wait for spring, is a present capacity that past environments happened to favor; the wager is structural and retrospective, placed by no one and paid out by the frequency of good environments [10]. This kills teleology cleanly without denying that things can be poised for a future, and it is the principle on which the anti-homunculus chain of Section 7 depends. Present-tense viability splits into three checkable types: staying in a shape (configuration), keeping a process above threshold (process), and staying able to act (capacity)—the three exemplified by the crystal, the flame, and the spore respectively (Section 11).

10The domain and its edge: binding as the wall, rest mass as its corollary

Every one of the four dimensions secretly presupposes a now. Boundary is an interior held over time; Drive is a rate per time; Memory is past constraining future across time; Integration is parts cohering over time. A now requires a timelike worldline carrying proper time—and carrying proper time in a way a viability function can be checked against requires being bound, localized enough to have a persisting interior at all. Binding is the real domain wall: an unbound, delocalized excitation has no interior to hold and nothing for a present-tense viability to be evaluated on. Rest mass is the corollary, not the criterion. A massless free quantum is the limiting case that makes this vivid: a photon travels at c in every frame, carries zero proper time, has no rest frame and no present, and so offers nothing for a viability function to check—it falls outside the domain (Figure 5). But a free massive electron shows that rest mass alone is not sufficient: it has a rest frame and proper time, yet unbound and spreading it maintains no interior and hosts none of the four dimensions. What admits a system to the domain is having a bound, localized present to defend; rest mass is necessary for that but does not by itself supply it. Saying where the wall runs is a result, not a gap.

Figure 5. The domain of persistence is strictly the timelike interior of the light cone. A timelike worldline carries proper time—a clock, a present from which past and future divide—and can host the four dimensions. The null cone is the edge: a free massless quantum carries zero proper time and has no present, and so is outside the domain.

The sharp form of the principle is not “massless things cannot persist.” It is free/null versus bound/timelike. Binding manufactures a rest frame even from massless constituents—a photon gas confined to a mirrored box has invariant mass and a rest frame. Binding always manufactures a rest frame, and with it a clock: any bound system has vanishing total momentum in its own frame, an invariant mass, and a proper time that advances. Whether binding also manufactures a screening boundary depends on the binding interaction. Screenable (electromagnetic) binding buys both at once — an inside and a present tense together, as in the hydrogen atom. Gravitational binding buys the clock but no screening boundary: by Section 8 gravity is the anti-boundary interaction, so a gravitationally bound system (a star, an orbit) acquires a rest frame and a present tense while its only boundary is causal, not screening. What binding universally supplies is the rest frame and the present tense; the screening boundary is the conditional extra. The domain wall therefore runs between the free photon (outside the theory) and every bound system (inside it), including bound systems built entirely of massless energy.

We flag this section’s central statements honestly. That rest mass is the price of persistence, and that binding buys the boundary and the clock together, are inferences from standard relativity plus the present-tense principle, not theorems anyone has proven. They are presented as this framework’s synthesis, and are not leaned on as foundation. Two objects sit at the theory’s edges as duals: gravity, the anti-boundary interaction, makes only the causal horizon; light, the anti-persister, is only ever the yardstick that horizon is defined against—the surface where even light cannot return. The horizon is where the two edge-objects meet.

11Five worked cases

The abstractions earn their keep on five concrete things, forming a progression from full persistence to none. A mode of persistence is a shape across the four dimensions in semantic weight (Figure 6); a transition is a change in that shape.

Figure 6. Three ways to persist, as semantic profiles over the same four dimensions. The crystal’s semantics are spent (frozen structure, no live drive); the flame’s are all in drive (sharp boundary, negligible memory); the spore’s are held in escrow (structure and integration stored, drive switched off). Bar heights are illustrative shape, not measured values.

A crystal is configuration whose semantics are spent. The living thing was the growth front—drive depositing structure; once grown, drive goes to zero and only the husk remains, memory made solid. Nothing it holds is load-bearing for continued persistence; it is terminal. A flame is process with its semantics all in drive: a sharp boundary rebuilt each instant from current conditions, with essentially no memory. It is the physical counter-witness that keeps Boundary and Memory from being welded by law; it is also the maximally observer-relative boundary (thermal, ionization, and luminous edges all differ), the opposite pole from the horizon’s partition-independent boundary. A spore is capacity with its semantics in escrow: drive switched off, structure locked, viability held in reserve and re-formable when a coupling re-forms. Its coat is a lock, not a reader; the germinant is the key; a dead lock still opens to its key. This is bet-hedging made physical—stored information with zero current viability value and high conditional-future value [10]—and it is what forces the choice of Memory numerator: the excess entropy E can read near zero on a richly structured process whose stored complexity Cμ is large [15], so two systems with identical E can differ arbitrarily in stored structure, and the spore’s semantics live in the latter.

A bound atom (the hydrogen atom of Figure 4) carries a boundary of each kind at once: a cheap screenable electromagnetic skin and an unscreenable causal horizon it is itself required to witness into being. It persists—briefly, modestly, really. A free photon marks the wall (Figure 7). It has no material skin, no causal patch of its own, and along its null path emission and absorption are a single event with no interior past-to-future; the four dimensions collapse together. The tempting objection—that a photon never decays, so is it not maximally persistent?—confuses duration with persistence. It resists no erasure, does no work to stay, and the duration is not even its own: it lives in an external clock while the photon’s proper time is zero, and even its one scrap of content, its frequency, redshifts toward nothing. It does not persist. It propagates—carrying persistence between the things that have it, being itself the edge where the notion stops being defined.

Figure 7. The four dimensions across all cases. The photon flatlines everywhere—not because it is a poor persister, but because it lies outside the domain of persistence entirely: there is no present tense to score it against. The interior cases (crystal, flame, spore) differ in profile; the photon differs in kind.

The five cases above are load-bearing: each isolates one claim (the flame breaks the Boundary–Memory weld; the spore forces the choice of Memory numerator; the photon marks the domain wall). A broader gallery of edge cases — a map of the ways a system can fail at persistence, from dissipative structures with no self to cultural and gravitational edge-objects — is provided as Supplementary Material; there, objects that fail the domain test (for example a black hole or a meme) are rendered at the wall, like the photon, rather than given interior four-dimensional profiles.

13Limitations and outlook

The framework’s honest exposure is concentrated in three places. First, the two soft dimensions—Boundary and Integration—are partition-relative, and Integration in particular has at least six inequivalent published measures that agree on rank but disagree on values, trends, and even sign [14]; any claim about the non-energy triangle inherits that ambiguity. Second, the semantic mask, though well defined as a construction, has its weights determined only relative to a declared viability functional; on any concrete substrate the couplings must be separately manipulable for per-edge weights to be defined, and on strongly integrated systems they may not be—which is not a defect to be removed but a fact about integrated systems to be characterized. Third, the observer-as-viability containment and Principle T are this framework’s inferences, secure only to the extent that the present-tense principle is airtight.

The productive next step is therefore not to add a dimension but to make the semantic mask compute on a minimal, deliberately resolvable model—one with an explicit, present-tense viability functional—and to measure not a single weight but the range a weight sweeps as the declared partition, resolution, and viability are varied. That range is the observable the framework actually predicts, since semantic weight is a quantity and Section 5 says quantities move with the grain. A clean single number would hide exactly what the theory says is there. The energy hub is coherent and defensible; the non-energy triangle is where the work remains; and the theory now knows precisely where its own domain ends—a bound system persists, briefly and really, behind a screening skin and inside a causal horizon it is itself required to witness; a free photon does not persist at all, but carries persistence between the things that do.
