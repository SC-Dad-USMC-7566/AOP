## INSERT 0 — top-of-paper caveat on the word "life" (place immediately after the Abstract, before §1)

> **A note on the word "life," read first.** This paper uses *alive* in a specific and deliberately
> **frontier** sense, and it should not be read as restating the settled standard of the field. The
> community's operational reference point — which we treat as **settled** — is the NASA Exobiology
> working definition, *"a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution"* [Joyce
> 1994; discussed in Cleland & Chyba 2002]. That definition welds two clauses (self-sustaining;
> capable of Darwinian evolution) and, by its authors' own account, scopes itself to chemistry for
> NASA's purposes. AOP proposes something different, on purpose. Its threshold (Section 11a) keeps
> the *self-sustaining* clause but (i) makes it **structural and substrate-independent** rather than
> chemical; (ii) is **present-tense and self-directed only** — it deliberately drops the *Darwinian
> evolution* clause, which concerns across-generation lineages and is placed above this framework
> (Section 9); and (iii) is defined as one specific coupling architecture — active self-maintenance
> against a decoupled internal model of the system's own viability — rather than by a checklist of
> life's hallmarks. We are clear-eyed about the standing of this: it is a **new definition, graded
> frontier**, offered as an internally consistent structural category (demonstrated for
> self-consistency, not tested against a rival — Section 11a), and it is **revisable or removable**
> if it does not earn its keep. Where AOP agrees with the NASA definition (self-maintenance is
> necessary) we say so; where it departs (substrate-independence, no reproduction clause, structural
> rather than hallmark-based) we mark the departure rather than smooth it over. *Alive*, here, is a
> proposal about structure — not a claim to have settled what life is. **[FRONTIER; stated as of
> July 2026.]**

## INSERT B — new Section 11a, "The living threshold," placed immediately after §11 (Five worked cases)

### 11a  The living threshold: continue, correct, and correct-against-a-model

The five cases fix the axes; they also expose a threshold the axes do not yet name. Three of the
persisters actively hold themselves off equilibrium — the flame, the star, and (were it running) the
cell — yet only the last is alive. The distinction is not *drive* (all three spend) and not
*correction* (both the star and the cell restore after a perturbation). It is **what the correction
is read against**, and it is a structural fact the framework can compute.

Three modes, separated by their restoring structure, not by their substrate:

- **Continue** — no restoring force. The flame is marginally stable: rebuilt each instant from
  current supply, it is gone the moment supply cuts, with no fight (Section 11). Its semantics are
  all in drive.
- **Correct, model-free** — a restoring force whose set-point *is* a fixed point of the
  constitutive dynamics. The star is the worked case: its negative-specific-heat thermostat pulls
  the core back when perturbed (Section 11), but nothing in the star *stores* the target
  hydrostatic state; the target is where the physics sits. There is no separable reference to
  intervene on. This is the settled cybernetic distinction between dynamic stability and regulation:
  a good regulator need not carry a model, and the ones that do not are simply the non-optimal or
  unnecessarily complex ones [Conant & Ashby 1970] — a bare stable fixed point is not thereby a
  model.
- **Correct, model-based** — a restoring force driven against a **decoupled internal reference**
  that stores the system's own viable set (its *essential variables*, in Ashby's sense [Ashby
  1960]) and is dynamically separable from the regulated variable it controls [Bich, Mossio,
  Ruiz-Mirazo & Moreno 2015; the internal-model requirement of Francis & Wonham 1976]. The cell is
  the case: setpoints are held in a regulatory sub-network, read out onto the fast physiology, and
  can be corrupted independently of the physiology itself.

We take **alive** to name the third mode: *a persister whose maintenance corrects the regulated
axes against a decoupled, separately-interventable internal model of its own viability.* This is a
threshold on the existing four dimensions and the mask, not a fifth axis — the model is a
load-bearing internal coupling from a slow reference onto the fast regulated dynamics, exactly the
object the semantic mask (Section 3) is built to detect. It is substrate-independent by
construction, and it does not relabel neighbouring accounts: autopoiesis names self-production of
components [Varela, Maturana & Uribe 1974] and is silent on a stored viability model; the semantic
mask's parent result concerns information a system holds about its *environment* [2], whereas the
model here is about the system's *own* viable set. The conjunction — a decoupled model of one's own
essential variables, maintained and read out — is this framework's synthesis, with the cybernetic
and organizational-autonomy results [Ashby 1960; Conant & Ashby 1970; Francis & Wonham 1976; Bich
et al. 2015; Moreno & Mossio 2015] as its settled antecedents. As stated in the note on "life" at
the head of the paper, we advance this as a **new, frontier definition** measured against the
settled NASA reference, not as established science. **[The definition of *alive* is FRONTIER — a new
proposal; its control-theory components are SETTLED; its internal consistency is SYNTHESIS, computed
(Figure LT).]**

**Computed, not only described (Figure LT).** The discriminator is made concrete on two minimal
closed-form (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck) systems that both correct toward the same essential target
μ\* and both restore after a kick. The *cell-type* system carries a regulated variable driven
toward the readout of a reference that relaxes twenty times more slowly than it does; the
*star-type* system carries two fast structural shells whose restoring set-point is baked into the
intrinsic drift, with no slow reference. Each internal coupling is scrambled in isolation and the
fractional drop in a present-tense viability functional (closeness of the regulated variable to
μ\*) is read as its semantic weight, exactly as in Figure MW; the generator spectrum supplies each
edge's timescale separation. The cell-type system contains an edge that is **both** load-bearing
(scrambling the model readout collapses viability and drives the regulated variable off target)
**and** decoupled (a twentyfold slow/fast separation); the star-type system's only load-bearing
edge sits at a twofold separation with the target still held by the intrinsic drive — it corrects,
but carries no decoupled model. The living threshold is therefore the joint condition *load-bearing
∧ decoupled*, and it correctly places the star — which the framework already treats as
corrects-but-not-alive (Section 11) — outside it. Like Figure MW, this is a demonstration of
self-consistency, not a test that could fail: the model edge is load-bearing by construction. Its
content is that the discriminator separates the two structures cleanly, is computable in the
framework's own idiom (mask weight × generator spectrum), and does not misclassify the star. Code
and data are deposited. **[SYNTHESIS, computed.]**

**Alive and reproducing come apart — the sharpest single case.** The threshold is self-directed and
present-tense; reproduction is lineage-directed and cashes out only across generations. The two
separate, and two mirror cases show it. The **virion** carries a pure "make-more-of-me" instruction
with no "keep-me-viable" clause — a copy-me blueprint with the engine off, aimed at propagation, not
self-reboot; it reproduces (in a host) and is not alive by the threshold above, having no decoupled
model of its own viability to maintain. Its mirror is the sterile persister — the **mule**, the
worker, the childless organism — fully alive by the threshold, with zero reproduction. The field's
working definition welds exactly these two clauses: "a self-sustaining chemical system capable of
Darwinian evolution" (self-sustaining = the present-tense clause; Darwinian-capable = the
across-generation clause), a NASA Exobiology working-group product articulated by Joyce [Joyce 1994;
discussed by Cleland & Chyba 2002]. The mule is a standing objection to that weld — the "Mule's
problem," that a sterile organism is alive yet cannot itself undergo Darwinian evolution
[Chodasewicz 2013, who states the objection in order to rebut it at the population level; even the
rebuttal concedes the *individual* mule does not itself evolve, which is all the separation here
requires]. AOP does not need to adjudicate the definition of life; it needs only the structural
separation, and the virion and mule supply it from both sides. **[SYNTHESIS; well-supported by the
definition-of-life literature.]**

**Spore, placed.** The spore (Section 11) is now nameable as *life paused*: model-based maintenance
switched off, the viability model held in escrow, restartable from the system's own held state. Not
a substance but a process-state — pausable and resumable — which is why the spore drops out of the
threshold for free rather than needing a special clause. The virion is its false twin: also
engine-off, but the held instruction is a propagation blueprint, not a self-model, so it never
crosses the threshold on reboot. **[SYNTHESIS.]**

**Death is not a fifth erasure.** On a system integrated enough that the axes are mutually
load-bearing, the four failures arrive together: drive-failure cascades into mixing, forgetting, and
fragmentation behind it. A crystal cannot die (it barely holds); a cell dies because it holds hard
on all four and the collapse of one pulls the rest. Death is the four dimensions failing as one on
an integrated persister, not a new axis. **[SYNTHESIS.]**

*Figure LT.* The living threshold, computed. Two closed-form OU systems that both correct toward the
same target μ\*. (A) Each internal coupling placed by its semantic weight (viability drop when
scrambled, the mask) against its timescale separation (slow reference / fast regulated, the
generator spectrum); the alive region is load-bearing ∧ decoupled. The cell-type system's model
edges land inside it; the star-type system's structural edge does not. (B) Cutting the cell's model
(readout or upkeep) collapses its viability into the low band; cutting the star's structural
coupling leaves it near-viable, its target held by intrinsic drive. Code and data are deposited.

## INSERT C — new Section 4a, "Diachronic individuation: the identity of a persisting process,"
placed immediately after §4's Φ_MIP individuation paragraph

### 4a  Diachronic individuation: when two slices are one process

Section 4 added a *synchronic* individuation coordinate — Φ_MIP, telling one individual from many at
a fixed time. Persistence also poses the orthogonal, *diachronic* question: when do two time-slices
belong to **one** persisting process? The framework's answer is not material and not descriptive. A
persisting thing is a process — a form continuously instantiated through changing material — and it
is individuated over time by **continuity of instantiation** (causal continuity of the process, the
genidentity criterion of process biology [DiFrisco 2018, in Nicholson & Dupré 2018]), not by
retained substance and not by a preserved description. This is a settled position imported, not a
new invention; what is this framework's is the mapping onto the four axes and the present-tense
principle. **[SETTLED named view; the mapping is SYNTHESIS.]**

Two contrasting thought experiments fix the criterion, and their difference *is* the result. In the
**transporter/de-extinction** case the process stops and a *description* is re-enacted elsewhere;
the result is a look-alike, a cover version, not the resumed original — the recreated thing is a new
process that happens to match a record. In the **Ship of Theseus** case continuity of instantiation
holds *through total material turnover*, because the pattern is never interrupted; it is the same
ship though it keeps no original plank. The two cases involve the *same* total substance swap; the
only difference is whether instantiation was unbroken (Theseus, same process) or
interrupted-and-re-enacted-from-a-record (transporter, new process). Continuity is unbroken
instantiation, not preserved material. This aligns with the deflationary reading of personal
identity on which there is "no further fact" beyond continuity itself [Parfit 1984]: identity is not
an extra ingredient to be preserved, it is continuity of the process, exactly what the genidentity
criterion asserts.

This gives death and its edge cases a clean reading, consistent with the present-tense principle
(Section 9). **Death = interruption of instantiation** — not loss of material (Theseus keeps none)
and not loss of the description (a frozen genome is intact). The mammoth species stopped being
sailed when the last individual died; its recoverable genome is a description, not a paused process,
so de-extinction would begin a new process, not resume the old. The operational test is present-
tense and needs no foresight: does the system restart *itself* from its own held state (spore →
paused, same process) or must something external rebuild it from a record (mammoth → new process)?
This is why *paused ≠ stopped* and *held-state ≠ description*. It also secures why death must be
real: if "the blueprint still exists, so it persists" were admitted, nothing would ever die and
persistence would mean nothing. **[SYNTHESIS.]**

**Fission is the honest hard case, and it is not new either.** One continuously-instantiated lineage
that splits into two leaves both descendants genidentity-legitimate; asking "which is the original?"
has no unique answer, exactly as in the fission cases of the personal-identity literature [Parfit
1984]. Diachronic identity is therefore not always one-to-one, and the framework does not force it to
be: speciation is a branching of one process into two, both continuations, neither uniquely the
original. This is a property of the criterion, marked, not a defect to be patched. **[SYNTHESIS;
FRONTIER at the formal treatment of branching.]**

## INSERT D — clarifying paragraph appended to §9 "Present-tense viability" (principle unchanged)

> Lineages do not breach the present-tense principle; they enter it at two admitted points and no
> third. First, *existence now*: a lineage exists now iff it is instantiated now — at least one
> member presently viable — a pure present-tense fact requiring no foresight, and one that fails
> exactly at the instant the last member dies. Second, as a *higher-order individual*: where a
> lineage is itself integrated enough to count as one thing (Φ_MIP > 0 at the lineage partition,
> Section 4), its net reproduction is a present-tense flux — a Drive in this paper's sense —
> maintaining the lineage-form off its extinction-equilibrium on the lineage's own clock (Section
> 5), and the lineage carries its own four-axis profile as any persister does. What the principle
> continues to refuse is the third move: reading a lineage's *future continuation* ("this lineage
> will persist") as a present-tense fact. That claim is not recoverable now and is the province of
> selection, one level above this framework. So a doomed but still-instantiated lineage has not
> "failed" now — its members persist, its doom is a future-tense selective fact the framework does
> not adjudicate, and it fails (stops existing) only when instantiation is interrupted. Propagation
> as differential continuation is thereby placed *above* AOP, at the scope wall where present-tense
> persistence hands off to the theory of evolution; it is a cross-project (Ladder) propagation-bus
> item, not a fifth dimension. **[SYNTHESIS; a propagation-bus item.]**

## INSERT G — frontier note at the scope wall: the recombination question (dated, removable)

Placed with the §9 scope-wall clarification (Insert D). Explicitly a frontier note about the
territory just *above* AOP, not a canon claim — included because it bears on how cleanly persistence
and propagation separate, and marked so it can be removed if the field settles.

> **Frontier note — the recombination question (status FRONTIER; stated as of July 2026; removable).**
> Just across the scope wall, one question about heredity bears on how cleanly present-tense
> persistence separates from propagation, and it is openly unresolved. Faithful copying degrades:
> without recombination, deleterious mutations accumulate irreversibly in an asexual lineage —
> Muller's ratchet [Muller 1964; Felsenstein 1974]. Recombination can purge them, repairing a poor
> copy against a good one and breaking the linkage that lets errors ride along [Felsenstein 1974].
> One leading hypothesis holds that the genetic variation recombination produces is a *by-product*
> of recombinational repair rather than its function [Bernstein, Byerly, Hopf & Michod 1985]; if it
> holds, it weakens the tidy reading on which copying is persistence while mixing is search, since
> mixing would then be persistence-of-the-blueprint throughout. But the maintenance of recombination
> remains a genuinely open problem [Otto 2009], and AOP rests on no resolution of it. The material
> sits above the scope wall — it concerns across-generation lineages (Section 9) — and is recorded
> here only to mark the boundary honestly. **This note is dated and provisional: it is to be
> re-checked against the literature on a fixed cadence and removed or revised if the field settles
> the question or moves away from the recombinational-repair reading.** [FRONTIER, July 2026]
