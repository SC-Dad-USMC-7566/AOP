### Task 17 — Reframe death as process-cessation-at-grain (M11 / RED-21)
**Locate:** lines ~561–565, the "Death is not a fifth erasure" paragraph.

This **dissolves** Aster's counterexamples rather than fencing them out. Intact membranes, persisting genomes, transient metabolism, and gradients decaying on different timescales are lower-grain processes still running at *their* grains — exactly what the reframing predicts when a higher-grain process stops. **Separate the durable claim from the contingent one.** The current sentence bakes in a causal cascade — "drive-failure cascades into mixing, forgetting, and fragmentation... the collapse of one pulls the rest." Keep it only as **one common route on integrated systems** ("on tightly integrated persisters the failures often arrive together, drive-failure typically leading"), not as the definition of death. The definition is cessation-at-grain; the cascade is a frequent pattern.

**Suggested rewrite:** *"Death is not a fifth erasure. A persister is a process with a present tense at a declared grain; death is that process stopping at that grain — not a new axis. On a tightly integrated persister the four failures commonly arrive together, drive-failure typically leading into mixing, forgetting, and fragmentation behind it; a cell dies this way because it holds hard on all four and the collapse of one pulls the rest. But lower-grain processes may run on after — membranes intact, gradients decaying slowly — which is expected: those are subordinate grains outliving the one that stopped, not the persister still alive. A crystal cannot die in this sense (it barely holds a present tense to lose). **[SYNTHESIS.]**"*

Keep the **synthesis** grade.
