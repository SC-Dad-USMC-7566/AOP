## 1. Claims about definitions, evolution, and learning

**M106-C01 — NASA's definition.** "NASA has its definition… the most commonly adhered to. …a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution." (Bartlett, L17.) **Verdict: accurate but incomplete. Grade: Settled** (that NASA uses this working definition). Wording matches NASA's pages; NASA itself vacillates between "the NASA definition" and "working definition" [D1 §4.1]. Provenance (1994 Joyce panel) is folkloric — the Joyce Foreword remains unretrieved, and Cleland & Chyba 2002 print a variant wording ("self-sustained… capable of undergoing…") — converging with the AOP LifeDef report's independent finding [D1 GOV-3]. Corrected: *NASA's widely used working definition, adopted for exobiology program purposes, is "a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution"; it is not a formally legislated agency definition and its exact original wording has two circulating variants.* Confidence high. L: pre-rung ch.1 already refuses single-feature definitions. A: the LifeDef rival-criteria matrix should carry the two-variant wording caveat.

**M106-C02 — Darwinian evolution = "heritable variation leading to reproductive success"; identical offspring = replication, varied = reproduction.** (Bartlett, L20–22.) **Verdict: accurate (informal). Grade: Settled core; the replication/reproduction cut is a lecture heuristic**, loosely related to replicator terminology, not standard taxonomy [D1 §4.1]. Confidence medium-high. L: matches the three-switch ratchet's variation clause. A: none.

**M106-C03 — "I'm unsure whether Darwinian evolution alone is sufficient to explain the diversity/complexity of life."** (Bartlett, L19–20.) **Verdict: contested (as science); accurate as a personal stance. Grade: Frontier.** Mainstream evolutionary biology holds Darwinian mechanisms broadly construed (including multilevel dynamics) sufficient; the insufficiency thesis is a minority/extended-synthesis position [D1 §4.1]. Corrected: *whether anything beyond Darwinian mechanisms (broadly construed) is needed for observed complexity is a live but minority research question, not an established gap.* Confidence high. L: the Ladder's spine already grades the deepening tendency Frontier — import nothing stronger. A: none.

**M106-C04 — Open-ended evolution: ALife systems saturate in complexity, and the cause is parasites piggybacking on stable replicators.** (Bartlett, L26–32, 38.) **Verdict: SPLIT — the saturation observation is accurate (Settled within ALife); the parasite-causation mechanism is incorrect.** Packard et al. 2019 (FULL TEXT): OEE "remains out of reach of today's ALife models," with no parasite mechanism among the identified obstacles. The two flagship primary sources say the opposite of the episode: Ray 1991 (FULL TEXT) — Tierra's parasites drove arms races and *maintained* diversity; Zaman et al. 2014 (FULL TEXT) — host–parasite coevolution *increases* complexity (EQU evolved in 17/50 coevolving populations vs 0/50 without parasites) [D1 §4.1]. Corrected: *digital-evolution systems do fall short of open-endedness, but the literature attributes this to model limitations, and its flagship results show parasites promoting rather than capping complexity.* Confidence high. **This is the audit's single largest factual defect.** L: do not import the parasite-saturation story anywhere; the pre-rung's parasite guardrail survives on other grounds. A: none.

**M106-C05 — Spiegelman's RNA: evolves toward "the minimal sequence capable of replication, and then they stop there."** (Bartlett, L33–35.) **Verdict: accurate but overstated at the end. Grade: Settled (minimization); the "minimal… stops" gloss unsupported.** Mills, Peterson & Spiegelman 1967 (FULL TEXT): 83% of the genome eliminated by transfer 74; the authors explicitly did *not* claim a minimal sequence was reached, and later work reduced it further (~218 nt, then ~50 nt — secondary-verified only) [D1 §4.1]. Post-2020: Mizuuchi et al. 2022 — a translation-coupled RNA replicator under serial transfer *diversified* into a five-lineage host–parasite network (post-episode movement). Corrected: *serial-transfer selection for replication speed strips Qβ RNA down dramatically (83% lost by transfer 74) and complexity does not regrow in that setup; "reaches the minimum and stops" is folkloric compression.* Confidence high. L: usable in pre-rung ch.8 as the canonical fidelity/selection-for-speed result. A: none.

**M106-C06 — The cooperation barrier; cheaters collapse higher-level organization; major transitions (Maynard Smith & Szathmáry).** (Bartlett, L38–47.) **Verdict: accurate. Grade: Synthesis resting on Settled sources** (primary body of MSS 1995 unread — abstract + secondary only; the substance is standard evolutionary game theory) [D1 §4.1]. "Cooperation barrier" is an informal label, not MSS's term. Confidence medium-high. L: direct contact with the Ladder's transition treatment; no new import needed. A: §9a's self-maintenance vs private-reproductive-stake split is the AOP-side formalization; contact noted in D5.

**M106-C07 — Group selection: "many biologists are skeptical"; intuitive group-vs-group argument.** (Bartlett, L48–50.) **Verdict: accurate but incomplete. Grade: Settled (that skepticism exists) over a Frontier formalism dispute** (Nowak/Tarnita/Wilson 2010 vs the ~137-author Abbot et al. 2011 reply — both ABSTRACT ONLY) [D1 §4.1]. Carroll's in-episode pushback ("isn't that just part of Darwinian evolution?") is apt. Confidence high. L/A: none new.

**M106-C08 — Sterile workers: "its genes are not getting anything out of that cooperation because its genes are not going to be passed on… one of the conundrums" for the Darwinian paradigm.** (Bartlett, L59–63.) **Verdict: INCORRECT as framed. Grade of the resolution: Settled.** Inclusive fitness has resolved this since Hamilton 1964: workers' genes propagate through related reproductives (Hamilton's rule br>c; Nonacs 2011 FULL TEXT; direct genomic evidence of kin-selected evolution in obligately sterile workers, Warner et al. 2017 FULL TEXT). Even the strongest published attack on inclusive-fitness *formalism* (Nowak et al. 2010) explains eusociality within standard natural selection [D1 §4.1]. Corrected: *worker sterility was Darwin's own puzzle and is formally resolved by kin selection — the live dispute concerns which mathematical framework is more general, not whether sterile workers refute gene-level accounting; "its genes get nothing" is factually wrong under all sides of that dispute.* Confidence high. **Second-largest factual defect.** L: the pre-rung/Rung-1 line must not inherit this framing; sterile-worker and colony cases belong in the two-stacks separation panel with kin selection stated. A: hostile-case panel entries "sterile worker" and "colony" (D5) must declare the lineage/colony level explicitly.

**M106-C09 — Evolution as associative learning (Watson); PAC (Valiant).** (Bartlett, L51–54.) **Verdict: accurate attribution, appropriately hedged in-episode. Grade: Frontier** (Watson programme — contested, with published critical response; Valiant's actual theorems are narrow and model-bound: evolvability = constrained statistical-query learning; parity provably not evolvable) [D1 §4.1]. Confidence high. L: if imported at all, as a boxed rival lens, never as spine support. A: "evolution as learning" is diachronic — above the scope wall by construction; see D5 §2.3.

## 2. Claims about dissipative structures, pillars, and cases

**M106-C10 — BZ history: "couldn't get published… no one believed… seemed to violate the second law."** (Bartlett, L71–74.) **Verdict: accurate in substance, mildly overstated in specifics. Grade: Settled (history).** Winfree 1984 (FULL TEXT) documents two editorial rejections ("supposedly discovered discovery… quite impossible") and the survival of the work only via an obscure 1959 symposium abstract; the community misreading was of detailed balance (which forbids oscillations *near equilibrium* only) [D1 §4.2]. Confidence high (structure), medium (exact wording/dates). L: excellent pre-rung color if used with the corrected thermodynamic point. A: none.

**M106-C11 — BZ recipe: potassium bromate, cerium sulfate, malonic acid, sulfuric acid.** (L72–74.) **Verdict: accurate** (standard modern recipe; Belousov's original used citric acid — Zhabotinsky substituted malonic) [D1 §4.2]. Grade: Settled. Confidence high.

**M106-C12 — "Selkov reaction… one A with two B gives three B."** (L75–76.) **Verdict: accurate but incomplete (defensible gloss). Grade: Settled.** A+2B→3B as an elementary scheme is Gray–Scott cubic autocatalysis; Sel'kov 1968 (FULL TEXT) is an enzymatic glycolysis model whose reduced kinetics give the same xy² term — Pearson's own *Science* paper calls Gray–Scott "a variant of the autocatalytic Selkov model" [D1 §4.2]. Confidence high.

**M106-C13 — Spot patterns "grow and divide… looks exactly like replicating bacteria."** (L76–77.) **Verdict: accurate but rhetorically overstated. Grade: Settled** (Pearson 1993; Lee et al. 1994 — abstract-level: "'birth' through replication and 'death' through overcrowding"). "Exactly like" is visual analogy only; no heredity/variation [D1 §4.2]. Confidence high. L: prime candidate for the pre-rung persister catalog (D4).

**M106-C14 — Bartlett's PhD result: coupled exothermic/endothermic spot populations regulate temperature and respond to thermal perturbation by shifting relative populations — homeostasis.** (L78–80.) **Verdict: accurate but incomplete; regime qualifiers missing. Grade: Frontier** (single-group, simulation-only, one artificial-chemistry family, 2-D lattice-Boltzmann; unreplicated). The published abstract (Bartlett & Bullock 2016, ABSTRACT ONLY) claims bidirectional boundary-temperature compensation, "completely emergent"; the thesis itself (FULL TEXT) hedges that the compensatory response is "not true homeostasis" [D1 FB-3/FB-4]. Note: the *paper's* region-8 claim that these spots "grow exponentially" is not explicitly demonstrated in any of its refs 52–55 that could be read. Corrected: *in 2-D simulations, coupled exo/endothermic Gray–Scott populations emergently held internal temperature near optimum under warm and cold boundary perturbations — a one-group, simulation-only result whose own author's thesis declines to call it true homeostasis.* Confidence medium-high. A: this is precisely AOP §11a's flame-vs-star distinction territory — see D5 §2.2.

**M106-C15 — "Martin Hangzik['s]" oil droplets show chemotaxis and solve mazes.** (L81–82.) **Verdict: misattributed (strict); accurate about chemotaxis. Grade: Settled.** Maze-solving with shortest-path selection is Lagzi, Soh, Wesson, Browne & **Grzybowski** 2010 (FULL TEXT; Hanczyc not an author; Hanczyc's own 2014 review credits Lagzi for it). Hanczyc's group: self-propelled droplets (2007) and salt-gradient chemotaxis (Čejková et al. 2014 — FULL TEXT NOT VERIFIED; maze-channel navigation seen only in supplementary-video/press coverage) [D1 §4.2]. Corrected: *maze-solving droplets are Lagzi et al. (Grzybowski lab) 2010; Hanczyc's group demonstrated self-propelled and chemotactic droplets.* Confidence high. L: if the droplet case enters the pre-rung catalog, cite Lagzi.

**M106-C16 — "Simple physical systems like sheared fluids can show hysteresis and memory."** (L112.) **Verdict: accurate. Grade: Settled.** Keim & Nagel 2011 (ABSTRACT) + Keim et al. RMP 2019 (FULL TEXT): cyclically sheared suspensions store training amplitudes as retrievable transient memories, noise-stabilized; the RMP deliberately avoids "learning" language — this is parameter imprinting, not associative learning [D1 §4.2]. Confidence high. A: benchmark-relevant (trained suspension = memory-without-semantics case; D5 §4).

**M106-C17 — Fire: metabolism-like, autocatalytic, "not homeostatic in the general case," doesn't learn, hence not alive.** (Bartlett, L117–119.) **Verdict: accurate (episode); note internal tension with the paper**, which grants fire "homeostasis of certain variables (burn temperature naturally stays within certain bounds)" while keeping it in region 3. Bounded flame temperature is a thermochemical constraint, not set-point regulation (Bich et al. 2015, FULL TEXT) [D1 §4.2]. Grade: Synthesis. Confidence high. A: AOP §11a already carries the flame as the no-restoring-force case; the v1.27 sweep corrected the flame's memory to "shallow, short-lived" — no conflict.

**M106-C18 — Learning definition: record + process + use such that utility/benefit increases; "natural selection is kind of like a feedback effect of that nature."** (Bartlett, L119–121.) **Verdict: accurate as a report of the paper's definition; the definition itself bundles semantics and diachronic success — see D3 §2 and D5 §2.3. Grade: Synthesis** (the definition is B&W's proposal, not established usage). Confidence high.

**M106-C19 — Trees communicate and share resources ("experiments… in British Columbia").** (Bartlett, L122.) **Verdict: accurate but incomplete for 2020; overstated by current standards. Grade: Frontier/contested.** Simard et al. 1997 (ABSTRACT): one field study, birch→fir net carbon transfer ~6% of isotope uptake, shading-modulated. Post-episode corrective literature — Karst, Jones & Hoeksema 2023 (+correction), Henriksson et al. 2023, Rillig et al. 2024 — finds field results too variable/confounded to generalize and **no peer-reviewed evidence** for preferential mother-tree provisioning [D1 §4.3]. The episode predates all of it; fault attaches to repeating the then-popular overreach, not ignoring later work. Corrected: *one 1997 field study found net birch→fir carbon transfer (~6% of uptake); whether such transfer routinely occurs via fungal networks, benefits recipients, or constitutes "sharing" remains unsupported by field evidence.* Confidence high. L: the pre-rung currently contains none of this — keep it out, or import only with the 2023–24 corrective attached.

**M106-C20 — Bristlecone pine: oldest known organism, White Mountains, ~4,000–5,000 yr; cones "activated by fire"; fire-resistant; framed as long-term tree learning.** (Bartlett, L123–128.) **Verdict: age accurate; serotiny claim INCORRECT; fire-resistance overstated; "learning" framing Speculative.** USFS FEIS (FULL TEXT): *Pinus longaeva* cones are **dehiscent and wind-dispersed, not serotinous** — fire-activated cones belong to lodgepole pine, jack pine, giant sequoia; the species survives only low-severity surface fire and persists partly because fire is rare in its sparse-fuel habitat; a living White Mountains tree aged 5,062 yr (Methuselah ~4,850) [D1 §4.3]. Corrected: *bristlecone is the oldest known non-clonal organism (~4,850–5,060 yr), but its cones open by drying, not fire; the fire-adaptation story told in the episode belongs to other species.* Confidence high. **Third clear factual defect.** L: do not import; if longevity is wanted as a persistence example, use the corrected version.

**M106-C21 — An older clonal "germline/community of trees… in California or Nevada."** (Bartlett, L130.) **Verdict: accurate but vague. Grade: Jurupa Oak Synthesis; Pando Frontier.** Best match: Jurupa Oak clone, southern California, >13,000 yr (May et al. 2009, ABSTRACT); King Clone creosote ~11,700 yr (Mojave; secondary); Pando is Utah and was age-unconstrained in 2020 [D1 §4.3]. Confidence medium-high.

**M106-C22 — Hydra: "immortal… don't have any programmed cell death"; "most organisms undergo programmed senescence, which means programmed cell death"; Martinez 1998 "controversial… don't know whether proven."** (Bartlett, L131–134.) **Verdict: core non-senescence accurate; "immortal" overstated; "no programmed cell death" INCORRECT; "senescence = programmed cell death" INCORRECT as a definition.** Martínez 1998 + Schaible et al. 2015 (both ABSTRACT): constant, very low mortality and stable fertility over 4–8 lab years = **negligible demographic senescence**, which is distinct from immortality; hydra possess normal apoptosis; organismal senescence means rising mortality/falling fertility with age (Jones et al. 2014), not programmed cell death; programmed-aging theories are a minority position; *H. oligactis* senesces after sexual reproduction (flagged, unverified here). The "controversial" hedge was fair to 1998 but under-credits Schaible 2015, available pre-episode [D1 §4.3]. Confidence high on the conceptual corrections. L: use only the corrected demographic-senescence version anywhere longevity appears.

**M106-C23 — Deep-ocean organisms "around for tens of thousands of years, maybe."** (L131.) **Verdict: overstated but right order of magnitude** — best documented: glass sponge *Monorhaphis chuni* ~11,000 yr (secondary-verified only). Grade: Frontier. Confidence medium.

**M106-C24 — Strong Gaia: "the whole of planet Earth can be considered an organism… activates mechanisms to keep itself in a comfortable state," with the hedge "I'm not saying I think [it] is true."** (Bartlett, L95–97.) **Verdict: characterization accurate; the hypothesis so characterized is contested-to-rejected; the hedge was appropriate. Grade: strong Gaia Speculative; non-teleological self-regulation Frontier.** Kirchner 2003 (FULL TEXT): homeostatic and optimizing Gaia are mutually inconsistent, partly refuted (ice-core CO₂/CH₄ feedbacks amplify), partly unfalsifiable; Tyrrell 2013 rejects Gaia (secondary); Lenton et al. 2018 (FULL TEXT) defends only non-teleological regulation via sequential selection, tests outstanding [D1 §4.3]. Daisyworld is not mentioned in the supplied transcript. Confidence high. L: the charter's no-teleology guardrail applies with full force; A: the "planetary phenomenon" framing lands on AOP's open level-selection problem — D5 §3.5.

**M106-C25 — Planetary radiation: "concentrated UV light coming in and less concentrated infrared light going out… a loss of free energy."** (Bartlett, L89–90, 100.) **Verdict: thermodynamic core accurate; spectral detail incorrect. Grade: Settled (corrected form).** Incoming solar is characterized by ~5,760 K emission, predominantly **visible/shortwave** (UV a minor fraction); outgoing ~252–288 K longwave IR; entropy-export framing per Kleidon 2009 (FULL TEXT; total planetary entropy production ~900 mW m⁻² K⁻¹). The paper's own wording ("low-entropy photons") is correct; the "UV" slip is the episode's [D1 §4.3]. Confidence high.

## 3. Claims about bioenergetics, origins, and information

**M106-C26 — Chemiosmosis: ions pumped at cost; return through "a molecular machine which turns… forms ATP."** (Bartlett, L137–139.) **Verdict: accurate. Grade: Settled.** Mitchell 1961 (FULL TEXT); Noji et al. 1997 (ABSTRACT: direct observation of F1 rotation) [D1 §4.4]. Confidence high.

**M106-C27 — "The most important metabolic cycle is the RTCA cycle."** (Bartlett, L139.) **Verdict: overstated. Grade of corrected form: Settled (distribution) + Frontier (origins role).** In extant biology rTCA is a carbon-fixation pathway of a limited set of mostly anaerobic bacteria (Garritano et al. 2022, FULL TEXT); the near-universal central cycle is the *oxidative* TCA; rTCA primacy is an origins hypothesis (Kitadai et al. 2017, FULL TEXT) facing Orgel's standing specificity critique (FULL TEXT), with only partial non-enzymatic support (6/11 reactions, Muchowska 2017; the 2019 iron network is not the rTCA cycle) [D1 §4.4]. Note: **the paper does not put rTCA in its Life definition** — the episode adds it. Corrected: *rTCA is one candidate first metabolism and a minority extant fixation pathway, not life's most important cycle.* Confidence high. **A clear overstatement (lesser roster, §5).**

**M106-C28 — Ribosome: common to all life except viruses; accretion history recoverable; "they have been able to reconstruct this very, very primitive, early, simple ribosome… which still has the ability to translate genetic information to protein sequence."** (Bartlett, L166–173.) **Verdict: universality accurate (Settled); accretion model accurate as retrodiction (Synthesis); the functional-reconstruction claim OVERSTATED to the point of incorrect.** Petrov et al. 2014 (FULL TEXT) is comparative-structural retrodiction with **no functional reconstruction**; Bowman et al. 2020 (FULL TEXT): decoding arrives late in the accretion model itself; the closest experiments (Bose et al. 2022, FULL TEXT — post-episode) get a protoribosome PTC vestige to form a **single uncoded peptide bond**, with no mRNA, tRNA, decoding, or templated synthesis. **No stripped-down or reconstructed-ancestral ribosome has been shown to translate.** [D1 §4.4]. Corrected: *structural comparison recovers the ribosome's accretion layers back to an ancient peptidyl-transferase core, and small PTC-derived RNAs can form uncoded peptide bonds, but no primitive ribosome has been shown to translate genetic information.* Confidence high. **The audit's flagship overstatement alongside C04.**

**M106-C29 — Genetic code origin "a deep enigma"; "why would a physical system invent a coding mechanism."** (L169–170.) **Verdict: accurate. Grade: Frontier.** Koonin & Novozhilov 2017 (FULL TEXT): stereochemical evidence weak; error minimization likely a by-product; the classic theories "have largely exhausted their potential" [D1 §4.4]. Confidence high.

**M106-C30 — Maxwell's demon "makes a measurement and is able to create free energy where there was none before. It can't do it for free… free energy cost involved in erasing its memory."** (L176–177.) **Verdict: accurate with a phrasing caveat ("create" → "convert information into extractable work"). Grade: Settled** (Landauer–Bennett; Toyabe et al. 2010 demonstration, ABSTRACT) [D1 §4.4]. Confidence high.

**M106-C31 — Information engines: molecules that measure their vicinity and change behavior, usable for energy conversion.** (L176–177.) **Verdict: accurate but incomplete. Grade: Settled (experimental).** Serreli et al. 2007; Wilson et al. 2016 (both ABSTRACT): information-ratchet kinetics transduce light/chemical fuel into directed motion; they do not generate free energy; "measurement" is implicit in position-dependent rate constants [D1 §4.4]. Confidence high.

**M106-C32 — Molecular walkers: "Their motion is not powered by the ATP. Their motion is powered by random fluctuations… if the kick is good, they… lock that position in by burning the ATP."** (Bartlett, L177–179.) **Verdict: overstated/contested — the pure-ratchet reading is not current consensus for kinesin. Grade: Synthesis (corrected form).** Ratchet-flavored models exist (Xie 2010, FULL TEXT), and diffusive search genuinely dominates parts of the cycle; but Hwang & Karplus 2019 (FULL TEXT): kinesin's force generation **requires an active power stroke** (ATP-binding-triggered cover-neck-bundle folding); mechanisms "not mutually exclusive." Thermodynamically, ATP free energy pays for directionality in either picture — "motion not powered by ATP" is wrong as stated [D1 §4.4]. Corrected: *motors harness thermal fluctuations, but directionality and force are paid for by ATP free energy through a mix of rectified diffusion and bona fide power strokes.* Confidence high. **Flagship defect (roster, §5).**

**M106-C33 — Multiple origins possible; single universal ancestor not definitive; "very strong limits on how far we can reach back… purely on phylogenetics"; communal early evolution.** (Bartlett, L157–159.) **Verdict: accurate, properly hedged. Grade: Frontier (communal specifics); Settled (phylogenetic limit at LUCA).** Woese 1998 (FULL TEXT). Post-2020: Moody et al. 2024 (FULL TEXT) — LUCA ~4.2 Ga, ~2,657 proteins, ecosystem-embedded; consistent with LUCA ≠ first life; neither vindicates nor refutes the communal-progenote picture [D1 §4.4]. Confidence high.

**M106-C34 — Viruses: "wrong to just say that viruses are not alive… they are an integral part of the biosphere… massively outnumber other organisms"; life as a system-level/planetary phenomenon.** (Bartlett + Carroll, L102–106.) **Verdict: accurate as a position statement; the abundance claim accurate (marine virology; Breitbart 2018 cited in the paper); the system-level move is the paper's §2 stance. Grade: Synthesis.** Confidence medium-high. A: this is the declaration-relativity contact — D5 §3.5.

**M106-C35 — Autocatalysis "is another way of saying exponential growth."** (Bartlett, L90–91; paper §2 definition.) **Verdict: contested — stipulative definition at odds with formal usage. Grade: the equation is B&W Synthesis; the counter-evidence Settled.** Blokhuis et al. 2020 (FULL TEXT): stoichiometric definition, no growth-law axiom; canonical non-enzymatic autocatalysts grow **parabolically** in their operating regimes (product inhibition; von Kiedrowski line; Sakref & Rivoire 2024, FULL TEXT — noting S&R also hold exponentiality to be extrinsic-conditions-dependent, so the "ideal conditions" hedge has a defense; see D3 §2 Q5 for the adjudication) [D1 §4.2]. Corrected: *autocatalysis is defined stoichiometrically; exponential growth is a special case realized when product inhibition is negligible.* Confidence high. A: D5 §2.4.

**M106-C36 — Homeostasis defined as "negative feedback mechanisms that rein in key variables" (episode) while the paper's region 2 grants homeostasis to an ideal gas at equilibrium.** (L92–97; paper §2.1.) **Verdict: the episode's version is the standard usage; the paper's equilibrium extension is contested — it stretches homeostasis to bare Lyapunov stability; the paper itself flags the subtlety (Landauer caveat paragraph). Grade: B&W's extension Synthesis; the critique's physiology core Settled, its architecture machinery Synthesis (organizational school)** (Bich et al. 2015, FULL TEXT: passive relaxation = dynamic stability; regulation = dynamically decoupled second-order control) [D1 §4.2]. Confidence high. A: direct contact with AOP §11a — D5 §2.2.
