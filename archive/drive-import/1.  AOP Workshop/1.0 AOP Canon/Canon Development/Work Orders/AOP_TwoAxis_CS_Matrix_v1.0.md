## 1 · The declaration, fixed before scoring

### 1.1 The V-rule (binding, uniform across all rows)

> **V(S) = the finite-horizon survival probability of the declared system S at its declared grain**, over horizon τ = 10× the slowest relaxation time of S's regulated coordinates. Member of the persistence-functional family (canon §1, §12″): **finite-horizon survival**. **R** (reversal convention, canon §12″): even, configuration-space variables throughout.

This is the prior arc's rule, unchanged and deliberately so. Changing the V-rule between arcs would make the two matrices incomparable, and the reassignment of the criterion (Ruling 2) only has an evidence base if the declarations match.

### 1.2 The intervention class **I** (binding, uniform)

**I** = { perturb a regulated coordinate away from its operating value; scramble or ablate a single internal coupling edge under the canon §3 internal-edge protocol; overwrite the stored content of a candidate reference while holding all rate constants of the regulated dynamics fixed; **and — new, and the operation axis A needs — remove or degrade the process alleged to produce a constraint, and observe at grain τ whether the constraint is replaced** }.

The fourth operation is what makes axis A measurable rather than interpretive. It is also stated so that it *can fail to be available*: where a constraint has no candidate producing process inside **S**, there is nothing to remove, and the axis returns *none* rather than *undetermined*. A change of basis is not in **I** (canon §12″, §11a).

### 1.3 What axis G is, and what it is no longer

Axis G is the previous arc's six-clause conjunction **reassigned, per Ruling 2**. Its operative test is the state-versus-parameter discriminator: *does the slow variable appear in the closed-form expression for the regulated target, or is the target a ratio of rate constants?*

**One clause does not travel with it, and this is the most consequential scoring decision in the document.** Clause (5) — *the reference's content is the system's own viable set* — was the clause that made a thermostat fail and a spacecraft pass, and it was the clause the prior arc found could only be defended by reaching for provenance. **On axis G it is dropped.** Axis G asks whether a decoupled, separately-interventable reference exists and is corrected against; it does not ask *whose* target it holds. The reasons, stated so they can be attacked:

1. *Whose target* is a question about ownership, and PIC's lesson — carried in the canon's refusals — is that measuring ownership directly failed. Retaining clause (5) reimports it.
2. Ruling 2 reassigns the conjunction to "a **different axis**." An axis measuring reference architecture should measure reference architecture.
3. The work now done by clause (5) is done better by axis A, and non-circularly: the difference between a thermostat and a cell is not that the cell's target is *about* the cell, it is that the cell *makes and replaces* the machinery holding it. That is a present-tense structural fact, not a provenance claim.

**The cost is paid openly: C1, the bimetallic thermostat, is now high-G.** So are a sprinkler system and a cruise controller. Under the incumbent criterion they were excluded. Under the two-axis structure they are low-A/high-G, in the same quadrant as Voyager, and axis A is what separates them from anything alive. A reader who finds "the thermostat is an agent" intolerable is objecting to the reassignment itself, and that objection should be made to Ruling 2 rather than to this scoring.

### 1.4 Reading the A-profile

Per axis, per case: **produced** (the constraint is made and continuously replaced by the processes it constrains, at grain τ) · **partial** (replaced in part, or replaced from precursors the system does not produce) · **none** (not replaced at all).

**The output is a four-vector and stays one.** It is never summed, averaged, or reduced to a scalar. Where this document needs a coarse label for tabulation it uses **nP**, the count of axes scored *produced*, and **high-A is defined as nP ≥ 3**. That threshold is a presentational convention declared before scoring, **not** a scalar collapse: every verdict below is stated as the four-vector, every quadrant claim in CS-1.2 is checked against the vectors themselves, and §5 NC1 shows a case where the threshold and the four-vector disagree in exactly the way the budget framing failed.

## 6 · Predicted failures — the negative-control requirement

The order requires **at least four cases where the two-axis structure returns the wrong answer**, with the right answer and why, and will return a matrix with no predicted failures unread. **Eight are named.** Declared before CS-1.3 and CS-1.4 run.

**NC1 · B3 mature erythrocyte — false negative, and worse than the incumbent's.**
Structure returns: `none/none/none/none`, low-A/low-G — **an exact tie with A8 NaCl crystal, D6 mousetrap, and A4 heat-killed spore.** Right answer: alive. Why it fails: axis A measures replacement, and the erythrocyte's whole biology is to run well while replacing nothing. The account has mistaken *one mode* of persistence — renewal — for persistence itself. Note this is also where the nP ≥ 3 threshold and the four-vector agree, so the failure is in the axis and not in the tabulation convention.
