## 0 · Five corrections to the order's own premises, before any scoring

The order says *cite these exactly; do not paraphrase the citations from memory*. Doing that turned up five defects in the order's §1.2 and in its list of authors' rulings. All five are reported rather than quietly worked around, and each changes what can honestly be put in a cell.

**0.1 The abiotic-minerals ruling is mis-attributed.** The order says "Cronin/Walker on abiotic high-assembly minerals — see Hazen et al., *J. R. Soc. Interface* 21:20230632, 2024." Hazen et al. 2024 is by **Hazen, Burns, Cleaves, Downs, Krivovichev & Wong** — byline read off the retrieved text; **no Cronin, no Walker**. It is an *attack* on assembly theory, not its authors ruling on their own case. Cronin and Walker's actual ruling is a separate paper, **Walker, Mathis, Marshall & Cronin, *J. R. Soc. Interface* 21:20240367 (2024)** `✓`, and Hazen et al. published a counter-reply, **21:20240622** `✓`. Both retrieved. Scoring R4's mineral cell from Hazen alone would have scored assembly theory on its critics' say-so.

**0.2 Friston 2013 contains no rock and no thermostat.** The order asks for Friston's position on "a rock, which Friston discusses." Mechanical search of the retrieved full text of *Life as we know it*: `rock` → **0 occurrences**, `thermostat` → **0 occurrences**. The rock is in **Friston 2019, arXiv:1906.10184, p. 83** `✓`, where an inert particle "can be said to show a simple form of self-organisation." **C1 and C2 are therefore scored *silent* for R5, not inferred.**

**0.3 Friston reverses himself on the candle flame, and the reversal runs against AOP's convenience.** In 2013 a candle flame **cannot** possess a Markov blanket, because the molecular interaction pattern is destroyed by gas flux. In 2019 (p. 50) the flame is "the canonical example" of blanket states in constant flux, and the difficulty is handled by an explicit simplifying assumption. **A6 under R5 is scored *contested-across-editions*, not as a clean exclusion.** Any AOP claim that the free-energy principle cleanly excludes fire rests on the 2013 wording and is contradicted by the same author six years later.

**0.4 The Gánti priority claim is half right, and the wrong half is the half prime leaned on.** The order asserts Gánti's absolute/potential split separates "what an individual must have to be alive now" from "what a system needs to found an evolving lineage."

- *First half — verified.* The absolute criteria apply to "every living being at every moment of its life without exception" — Gánti's own words, quoted verbatim with page numbers in **Ruiz-Mirazo, Peretó & Moreno 2010, p. 208** `✓`. **Priority over AOP is not in doubt.** **[SETTLED.]**
- *Second half — does not match the text.* The potential criteria are characterised as "indispensable with regard to the surviving of **the living world**" — not a lineage — and the list includes **mortality**, which no lineage-founding reading predicts. **Recommend prime restate the claim as: absolute = the individual alive now; potential = what the living world needs to keep going, of which lineage continuation is one of three alongside open-ended complexity growth and mortality.**
- *Edition defect.* The verbatim wording is from **Gánti 1987, *The Principle of Life*, OMIKK Budapest, pp. 68–69** — not the 2003 OUP volume the order cites. The pp. 68–69 locator must not be attached to the 2003 edition.

**0.5 Gánti on viruses could not be located in any source.** The order instructs that where a rival's authors ruled a case, the ruling should be cited rather than derived, and names "Gánti on viruses" as such a case. **No retrieved source shows Gánti adjudicating viruses.** The most likely home — Griesemer, *J. Theor. Biol.* (2015), doi:10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.05.016 — is closed at every open-access route `⚠`. What *is* documented is Gánti ruling that bare replicator mixtures are not alive `✓`. **A5 and B1 under R3 are therefore scored from that adjacent ruling and marked as extensions, not as Gánti's virus verdict.**

A sixth item, in AOP's favour and equally unasked-for: **R2 has an earlier-published version of the alive≠reproducing split than R3.** The 1974 abstract states the field's error was to treat reproduction as necessary, "hence, not to ask about the organization which makes a living system a whole, autonomous unity that is alive regardless of whether it reproduces or not." Published 1974 against Gánti's 1987 Hungarian-to-English record — though Gánti's untranslated original is 1971, so the priority may still be his. **Prime should resolve this before AOP cites either as precedent.** Provenance caveat: two different abstracts for the 1974 paper are in circulation and this seat has not seen the printed page; graded `~`.

## 1 · How to read the matrix

Each cell is a verdict glyph plus a one-token reason.

| Glyph | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ● | positive — the criterion returns *alive* / the case is on its positive side |
| ◐ | paused / second tier (AOP only) |
| ○ | negative |
| ◆ | **contested within the rival's own literature** — its adherents disagree, or a published application returns a result its proponents would resist |
| ? | undetermined — an evidential gap this seat could not close |
| · | **silent** — the rival's literature does not adjudicate this class of case, and this seat declines to extend it |
| × | out of scope by the rival's own gate (e.g. R1's word *chemical*) |
| ∅ | not well posed |

**▪ marks a cell where the rival's own authors ruled the case** and the verdict is cited rather than derived. **Every unmarked cell is this seat's extension of the rival to a case its literature did not decide.** There are 32 × 5 = 160 rival cells; **30 carry the mark** — R1 3 · R2 7 · R3 4 · R4 7 · R5 9. That ratio is the single most important fact about this matrix: **81% of the rival column content is extension**, and an extension is an argument, not a citation.

**A caution the order asked for by implication.** A chart flatters its author. This one was built by the seat whose criterion occupies column one, from a case set that seat did not choose but did score first. The rivals were scored by a track blinded to the AOP column, but the *mapping* from evidence to glyph was done here. **Where a rival looks worse than AOP, suspect this seat before believing the chart.**

## 3 · What the matrix shows

### 3.1 The rivals disagree with each other far more than any of them disagrees with AOP

Counting positives across the 32 cases: **AOP 8, R1 4, R2 5, R3 5, R4 15, R5 22.**

The spread is not noise; it is a difference in *what kind of thing each criterion is about*. R4 counts fifteen positives because it measures molecular ensembles and reads positive on anything containing high-assembly molecules in abundance — including a heat-killed spore, which its own authors state deliberately: the method "can detect living systems even if they are no longer active" `✓`. R5 counts twenty-two because, in Friston's own words, its criteria "apply to (almost) all ergodic systems encountered in the real world."

**Neither of those is a criterion for the living state, and both say so in their own texts.** The honest reading is that the six columns are not six answers to one question. R4 is a biosignature detector, R5 is a general account of self-organisation, R1 is a criterion for *life as a phenomenon* rather than for an individual, and R2, R3 and AOP are the only three attempting the same task: **what makes this system, now, alive.**

### 3.2 On the four Tier A paradigm cases that matter, the three comparable criteria agree

A1, A2 (alive) and A6, A8 (not alive) come back the same under AOP, R2 and R3. The order's pre-registration says the criterion survives only if it returns defensible verdicts on all eight Tier A cases. **It does, with one qualification and one failure**: A7 (star) is defensible and clean, A5 (virion) is defensible, but **A3 and A4 receive identical AOP rows** — the live/dead spore non-result recorded in the verdict matrix at §5 F4. R2 handles that pair no better (both score *production halted*). **R3 is the only one of the three with a principled route**, since Gánti's absolute criteria demand active metabolism, and metabolism is exactly what heat-killing destroys while leaving the wiring intact.

### 3.3 The two cases prime expected to decide the arc, across all six columns

**C3 spacecraft.** AOP ●. R1 × (not chemical). R2 ○ (no self-production). R3 ○ (no metabolism; and ~ solid-state gate). R4 ◆ (technology, which R4 explicitly does not separate from life). R5 ● (blanket + ergodic). **AOP and R5 admit it; R2 and R3 exclude it, and they exclude it on the same feature — self-production of the components that constitute the boundary.**

That is the sharpest comparative result in this matrix and it does not favour AOP. **The two rivals that are actually trying to answer AOP's question both have a principled exclusion of the spacecraft, and AOP does not.** The feature they use is one AOP declines to require.

**D3 central bank.** AOP ○ (the target is a state of the economy). R2 ◆ (contested — Zeleny & Hufford's published application of the six-rule key admits a human family as autopoietic `✓`, and the Luhmann social-systems tradition extends further). R3, R4 silent. R5 ● (blanket at its own scale). **AOP's exclusion of D3 is the cleanest single exclusion any criterion makes on Tier D**, and it is AOP's best result in this document.

### 3.4 The published false positives are on the rivals' side too

The order warns against using rivals' problems as cheap wins, so these are stated with their sources and their defenders' framing:

- **R2 admits an inorganic precipitate.** Zeleny & Hufford 1992 `✓` run Varela's six-point key point-by-point on Leduc osmotic growths — precipitated inorganic salt membranes — and conclude the osmotic growth "is an autopoietic unity in the space in which its components exist" (p. 154). The admission comes through the escape clause **built into Varela's own point #6** (a component not produced within the network still qualifies if it "participate[s] as a necessary permanent constitutive component"). This is not misuse; it is the key working as written. **Represent them fairly: Zeleny & Hufford regard this as a correct widening of "living organization," not as an embarrassment.**
- **R4 reads positive on dead material** by design (§3.1).
- **R5 admits nearly everything** and its author raises the demarcation problem against himself, answering only with the conjecture that "minimum entropy Markov blankets characterize biological systems" — his word, *conjecture*.

**AOP's own false positives, from the deposited matrix, are C3, C7′, C8, D2 and D5.** Five, against R2's one documented, R4's by-design, and R5's near-universal. On raw false-positive count AOP sits in the middle of the field, not at the top.

### 3.5 The genre warning lands

The order flags that R2's six-rule key "is a procedure for *applying* a definition, not a test it could fail," and that the subsequent literature is largely arguments about whether candidates satisfy the rules. **Verified, and the structure is decisive**: every one of the six points is imperative-mood addressed to an observer ("Determine if…"), and every failure branch disposes of *the candidate system* — "you do not have an autopoietic unity" — never of the criterion. Point #1's failure branch is epistemic ("the entity is indescribable and we can say nothing"), charging the observer's inability to individuate to the observer rather than to the theory. **[SETTLED on the form of the rules, from Zeleny & Hufford quoting Varela et al. at pp. 192–193 `✓`; the 1974 primary is `⚠` not retrieved.]**

**AOP's six-part conjunction has exactly the same form.** This is the genre warning the order intended, and this seat confirms it lands on AOP as squarely as on R2: a six-part conjunction with an analyst-supplied declaration tuple is a decision procedure. What would make AOP different is a case where the conjunction is determinately false and the thing is determinately alive — which is what §5 F1 of the verdict matrix (the erythrocyte) claims to be, and which is the strongest thing AOP has going for it as a falsifiable proposal rather than a definition.

## 5 · What this matrix cannot support

- **28 of 160 rival cells are citations; 132 are extensions.** No comparative claim resting on an unmarked cell is stronger than this seat's reading of the rival.
- **R2's six rules are secondary throughout.** If the arc turns on the exact wording of point #5 or #6, the 1974 primary must be obtained by interlibrary scan first.
- **R3's dormancy verdicts (A3, B4, D8) are a genuine gap**, marked `?`. Gánti's absolute criteria include active metabolism, which suggests dormancy fails; a secondary reports the split was designed to handle resting seeds and frozen tissue culture, which suggests it passes. **The two readings point opposite ways and the primary is unread.**
- **The frozen set is favourable to AOP in one specific way**, and the order asked for this to be said: it is heavy in *engineered control systems* (C1, C2, C3, C7, D6, D7) — the exact class AOP's control-theoretic vocabulary describes natively and R1 excludes by a single word. A case set weighted toward **origin-of-life chemistry** — protocells, lipid vesicles, coacervates, RNA-world replicators — would play to R2 and R3 and would strand AOP, which has nothing to say about a vesicle that grows and divides without a regulator. **The frozen set avoided the case class where AOP looks worst.** That is not an accusation against whoever chose it; it is the honest answer to the order's question.
