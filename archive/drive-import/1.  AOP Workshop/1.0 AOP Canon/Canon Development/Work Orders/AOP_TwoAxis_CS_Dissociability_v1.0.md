**Read the first row.** A NaCl crystal and a spacecraft fault-management system have **identical** A-profiles — both replace nothing on all four axes — and sit at opposite ends of G. **Read the second.** *E. coli* and (under the D2′ reading) a corporation have identical A-profiles and sit at opposite ends of G. ### 1.2 A is not a function of G

7 distinct A-vectors occur among high-G cases; 12 among low-G. Knowing G narrows the A-profile to a set of 7 or 12 possibilities, i.e. barely at all.

## 3 · The sparsest quadrant — structural or accidental?

**Accidental, and specifically an artefact of set construction. This seat is confident of that and says so, because it is the one place where the honest answer helps the structure rather than hurting it.**

The frozen 32 were selected for the *previous* arc — to stress a life criterion — so they are dense in things that are alive-but-not-agents and in artefacts-that-are-agents, and contain **no human, no cephalopod, no corvid**: the three systems order §1.4 itself names as the quadrant's occupants. Tier E added one real high-A candidate (E6 lichen, which is low-G) and two fictional ones.

**So the sparsity has a mundane explanation and a live worry, and they are not the same:**

- *Mundane:* the set was frozen for a different question and under-samples the quadrant. Adding a human would populate it immediately and nobody disputes the verdict.
- *Live:* every real high-A/high-G system anyone can name **got there by being alive first** — which is order §1.4's diachronic question, out of scope here, routed to the Ladder bridge memo and **not** answered by this document.

**What AOP owes is the synchronic dissociation, and the synchronic dissociation is delivered: at n = 1 on real systems, plus B7's verdict standing on `~`-grade evidence.** That is occupancy, and it is thin.

## 5 · Is either axis a function of the other? — verdict

**No, on both directions, demonstrated at identical values rather than by correlation.**

**The structure does not collapse to one axis. It is also not orthogonal, and Ruling 4 does not ask it to be.**

**What this seat will not claim.** That the dissociation is *robust*. It is demonstrated on a set that was frozen for another purpose, in a quadrant with one real occupant whose verdict rests on abstract-level evidence. **The correct summary is: the dissociation holds, narrowly, and its narrowness is measurable — n = 1.**
