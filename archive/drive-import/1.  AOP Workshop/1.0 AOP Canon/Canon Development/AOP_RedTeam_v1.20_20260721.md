## Executive summary

Three closures are premature.

First, E7 establishes a one-sided result for a narrow class of measure-preserving currents and computes a clean example inside that class. That part is solid on the record examined. But the star paragraph does not establish the positive proposition that fusion lengthens stellar persistence. It only establishes that the measure-preserving-current theorem cannot refute that proposition. “The theorem does not bind the star” removes a contradiction; it supplies neither a stellar counterfactual nor a causal mechanism. The added claims that fusion “reshapes the stationary state” and lengthens lifetime “through the forced Boundary and Memory edges” are not demonstrated by the theorem, the double-well computation, or the negative-specific-heat thermostat.

Second, the D→I passage rests its strongest scientific statement on arXiv:2410.13375 while the bibliography still says `[Authors TBD]` and expressly records that the theorem, assumptions, and definition of “robust” were not line-checked. Even if the development dossier’s summary is accurate, the result described is a necessity claim—strong maintained correlation requires dissipation—not evidence that drive tends to generate integration. The canon labels the edge a “conditional tendency,” but its anchors support at most an I→need-for-D constraint and a cost bound. That is a direction-of-inference defect independent of the bibliographic incompleteness.

Third, the “1 of 15” score is not a defensible closure count. Several rows counted as closed are analogical bridges, unproved ports between inequivalent measures, explicitly partial substitutes, or claims whose cited support is still unverified. A referee would reopen at least seven rows. The arithmetic conflates “we know what literature or computation to invoke” with “the scientific gap has been closed.”

## Finding 1 — E7 reconciles the star with a theorem; it does not establish stellar pro-persistence

### Exact claims under attack

E7 states:

> “A drive that appears to *lengthen* lifetime — a star’s fusion — does not contradict this: fusion is not a measure-preserving current at fixed stationary distribution, it **reshapes the stationary state** … so the theorem does not bind it; such a drive lengthens lifetime through the forced Boundary and Memory edges and a present dynamical restoring force (the negative-specific-heat thermostat, §11a), not through the measure-preserving lever.”

The v1.20 changelog then compresses this to:

> “the star reconciled as a stationary-reshaping drive the theorem does not bind.”

The combined report had described Phase B’s task more cautiously: decide whether “Drive lengthens persistence” is ever a clean statement. E7 treats that question as answered, but the material cited in E7 answers only the theorem-scope question.

### What is solid

The scoped result for measure-preserving circulation is not the hole. On the materials examined, v1.20 identifies the hypotheses, cites a one-sided small-noise result, gives the unstable-eigenvalue expression for its two-dimensional double well, and reports a deposited first-passage computation. Within that declared class, “circulation shortens or leaves unchanged MFPT” is a coherent claim.

The objection begins when the text crosses from that class to a star.

### The escape hatch does only negative work

Showing that fusion is not a divergence-free current at fixed invariant measure establishes:

> the one-sided theorem cannot be applied to the star.

It does **not** establish:

> fusion lengthens the star’s AOP lifetime, or does so by the mechanism named.

Those propositions are logically independent. A hypothesis violation makes either sign possible; it does not select the positive sign. The canon has converted “not ruled out” into “established by reconciliation.”

The double-well result cannot carry the missing burden. It demonstrates an anti-persistence effect in the theorem’s own regime. It contains no nuclear source term, changing composition, hydrostatic evolution, radiative transport, or stellar failure set. It therefore supports the measure-preserving half of E7 and is silent on the star half.

### “Reshapes the stationary state” is not yet a mechanism

The phrase is doing more work than its definitions can currently bear.

A main-sequence star is approximately hydrostatic and thermally regulated, but its composition changes secularly as fuel is burned. E7 does not specify the stochastic state space, stationary distribution, quasipotential, or stationary intervention whose “reshaping” is being asserted. A changing hydrostatic profile along a quasi-static evolutionary sequence is not automatically the same object as the invariant density in the Lee–Seo/Bouchet–Reygner result.

Without that mapping, “stationary-state reshaping” is a category label for why the theorem is out of scope, not a demonstrated stellar mechanism. It says the star is different; it does not show how the difference produces a longer first-passage time.

### The named Boundary and Memory edges do not imply a positive lifetime sign

E7 says stellar fusion lengthens lifetime “through the forced Boundary and Memory edges.” But v1.20 itself books Drive→Boundary as **conditional**, not forced. Its worked pump-and-leak result establishes a maintenance cost for holding a contrast, not a theorem that more drive strengthens a stellar boundary or lengthens stellar life.

The hard Drive→Memory result is also narrower: positive entropy production forces predictive information under the canon’s stated conditions. That does not entail that predictive information mediates a longer stellar lifetime. Nor does the negative-specific-heat thermostat supply the missing implication. A restoring response establishes local regulation or stability; it does not, by itself, establish a longer time to the canon’s erasure event under an intervention on fusion.

The three ingredients named in the sentence—boundary maintenance, predictive memory, and a restoring force—may all be relevant. No result quoted in E7 composes them into a positive-sign lifetime theorem.

### The counterfactual is absent

“Fusion lengthens lifetime” requires a comparison. E7 does not declare what is held fixed or what counts as the matched alternative: the same initial mass and composition with nuclear reactions suppressed; a contracting pre-main-sequence object; a star with a scaled reaction rate; or a different equilibrium sequence. These comparisons do not ask the same question and need not produce the same monotonic answer.

Nor is the erasure/exit set specified. Loss of hydrostatic equilibrium, departure from the main sequence, exhaustion of a fuel species, collapse, and cessation of luminous fusion are different first-passage events. Until the intervention and exit event are declared, the sign claim is not well-formed in the same way that the ring and double-well claims are.

### What evidence would settle this objection

The positive stellar claim would be established by a paired stellar-evolution counterfactual with declared initial conditions, a declared intervention on nuclear energy generation, and a declared AOP exit set, showing a longer first-passage time and identifying the proposed mediation. An analytic stellar-timescale comparison could establish a narrower claim if it is explicitly tied to that same intervention and exit event. Evidence merely showing that stars are self-regulating, have negative specific heat, or fall outside the measure-preserving theorem would not settle it.

### Verdict

E7 successfully removes a contradiction. It does not yet establish the star’s pro-persistence claim. The current wording books exculpation as mechanism.

## Finding 2 — E10 depends on an unaudited theorem and points the causal arrow farther than its support

### Exact claims under attack

The canon states:

> “Dynamically, drive is a *precondition* for a strong form of maintained integration but does not *force* it: robust, size-extensive multipartite correlation cannot be sustained in thermal equilibrium and requires far-from-equilibrium, time-dependent (limit-cycle) dynamics [necessity result, ‘Dissipation enables robust extensive scaling of multipartite correlations,’ arXiv:2410.13375 (2024)] …”

The bibliography entry is:

> “[Authors TBD]. Dissipation enables robust extensive scaling of multipartite correlations. arXiv:2410.13375 (2024). … [~ result verified; ⚠ line-check the theorem statement, the ‘robust’ definition, and confirm the author list before final.]”

The development dossier similarly records that the full derivation, permutation-invariance assumptions, and exact robustness definition were not checked.

### The canon cannot treat an unverified theorem statement as load-bearing

This is not a cosmetic bibliography defect. The title, author list, theorem domain, quantified variables, and robustness definition determine whether the result supports the canon’s sentence. The canon currently asserts a universal-sounding impossibility—robust extensive multipartite correlation “cannot” be sustained at equilibrium—while its own audit trail admits that the theorem and its key predicate were not verified.

The likely attack surfaces are already named in the source dossier: permutation invariance, the exact meaning of robustness, and the restriction to time-dependent attractors. Any one could substantially narrow the result. “Robust” might mean stability under a particular parameter perturbation rather than the cross-model or empirical robustness a reader will infer. “Multipartite mutual information” might be computed for a restricted family that does not map cleanly to AOP’s TC or Φ_MIP. “Only limit cycles” might be a conclusion inside a specific attractor/model class rather than a no-go theorem for equilibrium systems generally.

Until those points are line-checked, the canon has no controlled statement to cite.

### If the theorem weakens, Parrondo et al. does not rescue E10

The second anchor is a thermodynamic cost/trade-off relation involving information. Such relations can show that creating or maintaining correlations under specified protocols enters entropy-production bookkeeping. They do not, without additional assumptions, prove that robust extensive integration is impossible at equilibrium or that time-dependent attractors are necessary.

The canon itself recognizes this distinction with the sentence “drive must pay for integration but does not automatically purchase it.” Therefore, if arXiv:2410.13375 does not say exactly what E10 requires, the remaining citation supports a protocol-dependent price relation, not the claimed general precondition.

### Necessity does not establish a D→I tendency

Even granting the theorem exactly as summarized, E10’s grade and arrow remain overstated.

The claimed logical form is:

> robust extensive maintained integration ⇒ dissipation / far-from-equilibrium dynamics.

That makes drive necessary for a specially qualified integration regime. It does not yield:

> increasing or applying drive tends to increase integration.

The canon explicitly concedes that dissipation can destroy correlation. A necessary condition that is neither sufficient nor monotonic does not define a causal tendency from D to I. At most, the cited result excludes one region of state space: strong robust extensive integration without dissipation. Calling that exclusion a “Drive→Integration dynamic edge” imports a direction the theorem does not supply.

The cost citation has the same orientation problem. If maintaining a correlation requires expenditure, the correlation creates a demand for drive; expenditure does not thereby tend to create the correlation. “Must be paid for” is not “is promoted by payment.”

### What breaks if arXiv:2410.13375 fails the line-check

Four v1.20 commitments lose their only direct support:

1. The categorical equilibrium impossibility for robust extensive multipartite correlation.
2. The assertion that time-dependent/limit-cycle dynamics are required.
3. The classification of the dynamic relation as a cited tendency rather than an open, model-scoped question.
4. The gap-register claim that row 8 closes by citation.

The weaker claims survive: equilibrium correlations can exist without dissipation; some protocols impose thermodynamic costs on creating or maintaining correlations; drive does not guarantee integration; MaxEP and strong dissipative-adaptation readings are not relied upon. Those are not enough to reconstruct E10’s positive edge.

### What evidence would settle this objection

The record needs the exact theorem/proposition, its model class, its definition of robustness, its correlation measure, its perturbation class, and the quantifiers behind “only” and “cannot.” It then needs an explicit equivalence or implication connecting that paper’s observable to the AOP integration quantity invoked by E10. Establishing a D→I *tendency* would require separate intervention evidence showing a directional statistical or dynamical effect of drive on integration over a declared ensemble; the necessity theorem alone cannot do it.

### Verdict

E10 is not publication-ready. Its main citation is visibly unaudited, and the inference would still outrun the theorem even if the development summary proves accurate. The strongest defensible object on the present record is a narrowly scoped necessity/cost constraint—not a D→I tendency.

## Finding 3 — “One genuine item left” is an internal planning score, not a referee-proof gap assessment

### Exact self-assessment under attack

The combined report says:

> “Of the ~14 substantive open items … Exactly **one** requires genuinely new work.”

Its register then concludes:

> “**Score:** of 15 rows, **1 is genuine new work** … Six close by citation, five by synthesis/edit, three are small closed-form computations that apply settled machinery.”

The problem is not that every other row is false. Several are solid. The problem is that the score treats heterogeneous dispositions—correcting a reporting defect, identifying a citation, writing a lemma, porting a selector between measures, and running a toy model—as equivalent instances of scientific closure.

### Entries that are genuinely solid

No objection is manufactured against the following core results:

- Row 1: replacing the false raw-correlation wording with the already-computed raw and conditioned values is a genuine defect correction.
- Row 2: the TC nesting identity, if reproduced as reported, establishes that B5 is the cross-cut slice of TC; that algebraic point is closed.
- Row 7’s narrow half: the entropy-production/antisymmetric-sector decomposition has a credible settled literature basis. The attack is on the extrapolated no-cross-coupling conclusion, not on the decomposition itself.

These successes do not validate the rest of the closure count.

Rows 12 and 13 show a different inflation: a useful diagnostic or proxy is counted as resolution of the phenomenon it was introduced to diagnose.

### Why the score is structurally misleading

The register answers a project-management question—“Can the team identify a next move using existing machinery?”—and reports it as an epistemic question—“How many scientific gaps remain?” Those are not equivalent.

- A **citation lead** is not closure until the cited proposition, assumptions, and target observable match the canon claim.
- A **synthesis** is not closure when it contains the very bridge a referee will demand be proved.
- A **small computation** closes a worked case, not necessarily the general edge that motivated it.
- A **partial proxy with an admitted residual** cannot be counted as a closed gap without changing what “gap” means.

On the register’s own documentation, rows 8 and 13 are explicitly not closed in the strong sense: one is `FRONTIER/tendency` with an unverified theorem; the other is `SETTLED + residual`. Counting both among the fourteen non-new-work closures makes “1 of 15” an accounting convention, not a scientific result.

### What evidence would settle this objection

The self-assessment would become defensible only if each row used an explicit closure criterion tied to the original question: exact theorem match, demonstrated equivalence of measures, scoped worked-case completion, or acknowledged residual. The count would then have to distinguish “closed generally,” “closed in a named model,” “reframed but untested,” “proxy available,” and “open.” Without those distinctions, no single fraction has a stable meaning.

### Verdict

The team has reduced uncertainty and corrected real defects. It has not shown that only one genuine scientific item remains. The current score materially understates the number of claims that remain conditional, model-bound, or dependent on unproved bridges.

## Final red-team judgment

The most serious issue is not any one missing citation. It is a recurring closure pattern:

1. establish a narrow theorem or worked model;
2. identify the flagship case as outside that theorem, or identify a neighboring literature result;
3. book the conceptual gap as reconciled or synthesized;
4. carry the closure into the version-level self-assessment.

That pattern is visible in all three targets. For the star, out-of-scope becomes positive mechanism. For D→I, necessity/cost becomes a tendency arrow. For the gap register, a plausible next move becomes closure. The underlying narrow results can remain true while the version-level conclusion is still overstated.

**Red-team disposition:** E7’s measure-preserving-current result survives; the stellar pro-persistence reconciliation does not. E10’s conservative disclaimers survive; its positive D→I tendency does not. The “1 of 15” claim should be treated as an internal workload estimate, not a referee-facing scientific assessment.
